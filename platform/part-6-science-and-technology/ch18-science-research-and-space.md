## Chapter 18: Science, Research & Space

### 18.1 Guiding Principle

Science is humanity's pursuit of truth.

Scientific discovery is among the most powerful drivers of:

- Economic growth

- Medical advancement

- National security

- Human prosperity

- Technological innovation

Government should support the creation of knowledge while preserving the freedom necessary for scientific inquiry.

### 18.2 Science, Research & Innovation Leadership Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain global leadership in scientific research, technological innovation, and advanced development.

#### 18.2.1 National Research Expansion

Federal support shall prioritize:

- Artificial intelligence

- Biotechnology

- Advanced materials

- Quantum systems

- Energy technologies

- Medical research

- Agricultural science

Funding shall be awarded through independent merit-based review.

#### 18.2.2 Basic Research Protection Principle

A dedicated share of federal research expenditures shall support basic research.

Many transformative innovations originate in discoveries that initially lack obvious commercial application.

Government investment exists to preserve the discovery pipeline that private markets alone may underfund.

#### 18.2.3 Scientific Independence Safeguard

Research decisions shall be guided by:

- Scientific merit

- Peer review

- Evidence

rather than political ideology.

The integrity of scientific institutions is essential to public trust.

### 18.3 Innovation Commercialization Initiative \[Statute\]

Scientific discoveries should create both knowledge and opportunity.

Programs shall support:

- Technology transfer

- Startup formation

- Research commercialization

- University-industry partnerships

- Applied innovation initiatives

The objective is helping discoveries made in America become businesses built in America.

### 18.4 National Laboratory Modernization \[Statute\]

Federal laboratories shall maintain modern facilities capable of supporting world-class research.

Investment priorities include:

- Advanced computing

- Energy systems

- Materials science

- Defense research

- Health research

### 18.5 Open Science & Public Access \[Statute\]

Research primarily funded by taxpayers shall eventually become publicly accessible.

Reasonable publication protections may exist.

However, scientific knowledge funded by the public should ultimately benefit the public.

### 18.6 Strategic Emerging Technologies Program \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Priority technologies include:

- Artificial intelligence

- Advanced robotics

- Quantum technologies

- Fusion energy

- Advanced nuclear systems

- Semiconductor manufacturing

- Biotechnology

These technologies are expected to shape future economic and geopolitical competitiveness.

Section 21.5 governs domestic production capacity in these fields; this section governs research support.

### 18.7 Scientific Freedom Principle

Scientific advancement requires:

- Open inquiry

- Academic independence

- Intellectual freedom

- Evidence-based reasoning

Government should support science without attempting to predetermine scientific conclusions.

### 18.8 Space Guiding Principle

The expansion of human knowledge is a public good.

Space exploration advances:

- Science

- Technology

- Education

- Economic development

- National security

Exploration is both a practical endeavor and a long-term investment in humanity's future.

### 18.9 Science, Space & Discovery Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain leadership in:

- Space research

- Space technology

- Planetary science

- Human exploration

- Commercial space development

#### 18.9.1 National Space Leadership

The nation shall continue investing in:

- Deep-space research

- Space science

- Earth observation

- Communications infrastructure

- Exploration technologies

#### 18.9.2 NASA-Space Force Research Partnership

NASA, the United States Space Force, universities, national laboratories, and private industry shall cooperate on research initiatives that expand scientific understanding and technological capability.

NASA shall remain the nation's primary civilian scientific agency.

Space Force participation in these research partnerships shall support infrastructure resilience, navigation, communications security, and space-domain awareness. Space security and resilience programs themselves are governed by Section 24.8.

#### 18.9.3 Public-Private Space Development

Federal policy shall encourage partnerships between:

- NASA

- Universities

- Startups

- Aerospace firms

- Research institutions

to accelerate discovery and innovation.

#### 18.9.4 Long-Term Exploration Goals

National exploration goals may include:

- Lunar research facilities

- Deep-space scientific missions

- Human exploration programs

- Planetary research initiatives

The objective is expanding human knowledge and technological capability.

### 18.10 Scientific Knowledge Principle

Economic development is important.

Scientific understanding is equally important.

Human civilization benefits when knowledge expands, even when immediate commercial benefit is not obvious.
