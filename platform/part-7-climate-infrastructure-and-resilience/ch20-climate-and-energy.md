## Chapter 20: Climate & Energy

### 20.1 Guiding Principle

Environmental stewardship and economic prosperity are not mutually exclusive.

The United States should pursue policies that reduce pollution, strengthen energy security, encourage technological innovation, and preserve environmental resources while protecting workers, consumers, and local communities from unnecessary economic disruption.

The purpose of climate policy is not merely reducing emissions.

It is to build a more resilient, technologically advanced, and economically competitive nation.

### 20.2 A Price on Pollution \[Statute – High Resistance\]

The United States shall establish a predictable and gradually increasing fee on carbon dioxide and methane emissions.

The fee shall be collected at the point where fossil fuels enter the economy, including:

- Oil wells

- Natural gas production

- Coal extraction

- Major import terminals

Methane released through leaks, venting, and decomposition rather than combustion cannot be priced at the point of entry. A measured methane fee shall therefore apply to major sources, including oil and gas operations, landfills, and manure lagoons at the largest confined animal feeding operations, under federal measurement and reporting standards. Methane from livestock digestion and from family farms shall be reduced through paid, voluntary incentives under Section 10.1.6, not through the fee.

Fee revenue shall be divided by statute, with each share used only as stated: 80 percent to the household dividend (Section 20.2.1), 10 percent to the Transition Fund (Section 20.2.2), and 10 percent to the General Fund for one-time or time-limited costs only (Section 27.3).

The objective is to incorporate environmental costs into market decisions while allowing businesses and consumers flexibility in how they respond.

#### 20.2.1 Household Dividend

Eighty percent of carbon- and methane-fee revenue, together with all Carbon Border Protection Tariff receipts, shall be returned directly to citizens through equal per-person dividend payments.

The dividend system shall:

- Protect consumer purchasing power

- Offset higher energy costs

- Ensure lower-income households are not disproportionately affected

Climate policy should not place excessive burdens on working families.

#### 20.2.2 Expanded Transition Fund

Ten percent of carbon- and methane-fee revenue shall support communities affected by energy-sector transitions. The Transition Fund shall end on a date fixed in statute.

Eligible investments include:

- Workforce retraining

- Infrastructure modernization

- Business development

- Community revitalization

- Economic diversification

- Educational programs

**Transition Community Principle**

Workers and communities that helped power the nation's prosperity should not be abandoned during periods of economic transformation.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 21 Infrastructure, Industry & Supply Chains.

**Honest limit:** Carbon pricing has failed repeatedly in Congress. The fee raises energy prices directly, while the dividend arrives separately, and households with high energy use, such as rural households and those in cold climates, may pay more than they receive. The fee must keep rising to work, which is politically hard to sustain. Revenue shrinks as emissions fall, which is why it pays for the dividend and one-time costs rather than recurring programs.

### 20.3 Carbon Border Protection Tariff \[Statute – High Resistance\]

To prevent carbon leakage and unfair competition, imported goods produced under weaker environmental standards may be subject to border adjustments.

The tariff shall apply only to the following industries, and may be extended to others only by statute:

- Steel

- Aluminum

- Cement

Tariff receipts shall be paid out through the household dividend (Section 20.2.1), not to the domestic industries the tariff protects.

The objective is preserving domestic competitiveness while encouraging cleaner production globally.

**Honest limit:** Measuring the emissions embedded in imported steel, aluminum, and cement is administratively complex. The tariff must be designed to comply with trade rules, and trading partners may retaliate.

### 20.4 Energy System Modernization \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain a reliable, affordable, and resilient energy system.

Federal support shall include public investment in research, manufacturing, and deployment of:

- Grid modernization

- Energy storage

- Advanced nuclear power

- Wind, solar, and hydroelectric generation

- Transmission expansion

- Research and development

The purpose is energy abundance, reliability, and long-term stability.

Because the best mix of wind, solar, hydroelectric, and nuclear power differs by region, federal planning shall support regionally tailored generation mixes connected by interregional transmission. Public investment shall aim to lower the cost and increase the supply of clean energy.

### 20.5 Advanced Nuclear Deployment \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Advanced nuclear technologies may play a critical role in achieving:

- Long-term energy security

- Grid reliability

- Low-emission electricity generation

Federal policy shall support:

- Research

- Demonstration projects

- Licensing modernization

- Workforce development

while maintaining strong safety standards.

**Honest limit:** Nuclear projects have a long history of cost overruns and delays, and advanced reactor designs have little commercial operating history in the United States. Faster licensing must not weaken safety review, and the country still has no permanent solution for storing nuclear waste.

### 20.6 Critical Clean Energy Infrastructure \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Nationally significant energy infrastructure shall be eligible for the coordinated review in Section 21.9, which reduces duplication and delay without weakening environmental or safety review. Eligible projects may include:

- Interstate transmission systems

- Advanced nuclear facilities

- Strategic energy infrastructure

**Honest limit:** Interstate transmission lines cross many states and private properties. Coordinated federal review does not remove state siting authority, and giving the federal government backstop siting power would face strong opposition from states. Approved projects can still be delayed by litigation.

### 20.7 Conservation & Environmental Stewardship \[Statute\]

Federal conservation policy shall emphasize:

- Clean water

- Healthy soil

- Wildlife habitat

- Forest health

- Watershed protection

Whenever possible, federal programs should promote voluntary participation and partnership rather than command-and-control regulation.
