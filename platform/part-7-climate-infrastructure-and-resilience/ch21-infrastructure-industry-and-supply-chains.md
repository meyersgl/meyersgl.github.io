## Chapter 21: Infrastructure, Industry & Supply Chains

### 21.1 Guiding Principle

Infrastructure and productive capacity are the foundations of national prosperity.

Markets create growth, but growth depends upon reliable transportation networks, energy systems, communications infrastructure, supply chains, and productive industrial capacity.

A modern economy requires both.

### 21.2 National Infrastructure & Strategic Capacity Act \[Statute\]

Federal policy shall support long-term investment in infrastructure and productive capacity necessary for future economic growth.

#### 21.2.1 National Infrastructure Renewal Program

Investment priorities include:

- Roads

- Bridges

- Ports

- Airports

- Freight rail

- Inland waterways

- Public transit

- Water systems

- Wastewater infrastructure

Infrastructure spending should emphasize long-term maintenance and modernization.

#### 21.2.2 State Partnership Framework

Federal programs should operate through cooperative state and local partnerships whenever practical.

Funding decisions shall be guided by:

- Need

- Economic impact

- Safety

- Resilience

rather than political considerations.

### 21.3 Grid Modernization & Energy Reliability \[Statute\]

The electrical grid shall be modernized to improve:

- Reliability

- Capacity

- Cybersecurity

- Interregional connectivity

- Disaster resilience

Priority shall be given to projects improving national energy stability.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 20 Climate & Energy.

### 21.4 Public Community Internet \[Statute – High Resistance\]

States may not prohibit municipalities from constructing or operating public broadband infrastructure.

Communities shall retain the right to establish publicly owned broadband services whenever local residents determine such systems are necessary.

Broadband access shall be treated as essential economic infrastructure.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 21 Infrastructure, Industry & Supply Chains.

### 21.5 Strategic Industrial Capacity Initiative \[Statute – High Resistance\]

The United States shall maintain domestic capabilities in strategically important industries.

Priority sectors include:

- Semiconductors

- Biotechnology

- Pharmaceuticals

- Aerospace

- Robotics

- Advanced manufacturing

- Nuclear technologies

The objective is resilience rather than economic isolation.

#### 21.5.1 Competitive Neutrality Principle

Government support shall remain open to firms of varying size whenever possible.

Industrial policy should strengthen competition rather than entrench existing monopolies.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 9 Competition & Consumer Rights.

### 21.6 Supply Chain Resilience Program \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall identify vulnerabilities within supply chains affecting:

- National security

- Public health

- Food security

- Energy reliability

- Manufacturing

The government may support:

- Strategic reserves

- Diversified sourcing

- Domestic production capacity

when excessive dependence creates national risk.

This section is the central supply-chain program. Sections 21.6.1, 11.2.4, and 22.6 apply it to domestic sourcing, trade treatment, and emergency reserves.

#### 21.6.1 Strategic Domestic Production \[Statute\]

Critical emergency supplies shall maintain domestic production capacity. The list of critical supplies shall be narrow and statutorily defined, recommended by a technical office at the national laboratories using a published method rather than by industry petition, issued by rule subject to congressional review, required to meet the Strategic Protection Test, and aligned with the vulnerability reviews in Section 21.6 and the reserves in Section 22.6.

### 21.7 Workforce Development Partnership \[Statute\]

Infrastructure and industrial investment shall be coordinated with workforce development.

Programs may include:

- Apprenticeships

- Community-college partnerships

- Trade certification programs

- Employer-sponsored training

Cross-reference: See Chapter 3 Work, Wages & Family.

### 21.8 Domestic Production Preference \[Statute\]

Federal projects receiving major public support should prioritize domestic production whenever:

- Quality remains competitive

- Price differences remain reasonable

- Supply needs can be met

The goal is strengthening domestic productive capacity while maintaining competition. Preferences under this section shall meet the Strategic Protection Test.

Cross-reference: Section 21.6 is the central supply-chain program.

### 21.9 National Permitting Reform \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Major infrastructure projects shall receive coordinated review procedures supported by funded permitting staff. This section is the platform's single permitting rule; Sections 8.1.2 and 20.6 apply it to housing and to energy infrastructure.

**One Project, One Review**

Federal agencies shall coordinate reviews to reduce duplication while preserving:

- Environmental protections

- Public safety standards

- Property-right protections

Environmental and safety reviews shall not be weakened, and public participation shall be preserved. Delays shall be reduced through funded permitting staff, coordinated agency review, and clear timelines, not by narrowing the scope of review.

Efficient permitting is not the same as weaker standards.
