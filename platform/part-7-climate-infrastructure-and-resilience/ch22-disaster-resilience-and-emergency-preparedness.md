## Chapter 22: Disaster Resilience & Emergency Preparedness

### 22.1 Guiding Principle

The most effective disaster response begins before disaster strikes.

Preparedness, resilience, and mitigation save lives, reduce economic damage, and improve recovery outcomes.

Federal disaster policy should focus on both response and prevention.

### 22.2 National Resilience & Emergency Preparedness Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain the capacity to prepare for, respond to, and recover from major emergencies.

Covered events include:

- Natural disasters

- Pandemics

- Cyberattacks

- Infrastructure failures

- Supply-chain disruptions

- National emergencies

### 22.3 National Risk Assessment Program \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall maintain continuous assessments of major national risks.

Assessments shall guide:

- Resource allocation

- Preparedness planning

- Infrastructure investment

- Interagency coordination

### 22.4 Critical Infrastructure Resilience Initiative \[Statute\]

Priority sectors include:

- Energy

- Water

- Transportation

- Communications

- Healthcare

- Data systems

Infrastructure planning shall emphasize redundancy and continuity.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 21 Infrastructure, Industry & Supply Chains.

### 22.5 Disaster Mitigation First Policy \[Statute\]

Federal programs should prioritize projects that reduce future disaster costs.

Eligible activities include:

- Flood protection

- Wildfire mitigation

- Storm hardening

- Earthquake retrofits

- Coastal resilience

**Prevention Investment Standard**

Mitigation investments should be favored when evidence demonstrates long-term cost savings relative to repeated recovery expenditures.

### 22.6 Emergency Supply Reserve System \[Statute\]

The federal government shall maintain strategic reserves of critical emergency supplies.

Priority categories include:

- Medical equipment

- Essential medications

- Fuel reserves

- Communications equipment

- Water-treatment supplies

Regular inventory rotation shall ensure readiness.

Reserve categories shall align with the critical-supplies list in Section 21.6.1 and the vulnerability findings under Section 21.6.

### 22.7 Public Health Emergency Preparedness \[Statute\]

Permanent preparedness capabilities shall include:

- Medical surge capacity

- Laboratory readiness

- Vaccine infrastructure

- Emergency logistics systems

- Public-health workforce development

Cross-reference: See Chapter 5 Universal Health Insurance.

### 22.8 Rapid Disaster Response Framework \[Statute\]

Certain emergency declarations may trigger automatic deployment of:

- Search-and-rescue assets

- Emergency logistics

- Temporary housing support

- Medical assistance

- Infrastructure-repair funding

The objective is reducing bureaucratic delays during crises.

### 22.9 Community Resilience Partnerships \[Statute\]

Federal grants shall support local preparedness efforts.

Eligible projects include:

- Emergency planning

- Evacuation systems

- Shelter facilities

- Backup power systems

- Communications modernization

Local communities often possess the strongest understanding of regional risks.

### 22.10 Build Back Stronger Standard \[Statute\]

Federal reconstruction programs shall incorporate resilience improvements whenever practical.

The objective is reducing repeated losses rather than repeatedly rebuilding vulnerable infrastructure in the same manner.

### 22.11 Continuity of Government Standards \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall maintain plans ensuring continuation of critical functions during emergencies.

Priority functions include:

- Public safety

- Benefit administration

- Health coordination

- Financial-system stability

- National security operations
