## Chapter 23: Foreign Policy & Diplomacy

### 23.1 Guiding Principle

Lasting security is built not only through military strength, but through diplomacy, partnership, economic cooperation, and mutual trust.

The United States should seek a world in which more nations view America as a trusted partner than as an adversary.

Military power remains an essential instrument of national defense, but diplomacy should remain the first instrument of foreign policy whenever practical.

### 23.2 Defensive Foreign Policy \[Statute / Executive\]

The United States shall maintain military capabilities sufficient to:

- Defend the nation

- Protect allies

- Secure global trade

- Deter aggression

The nation shall end foreign regime-change operations and shall reduce reliance on:

- Long-term occupations

- Open-ended military engagements

whenever practical.

**Offshore Balancing Posture**

The United States shall shift from permanent foreign land bases toward maritime and over-the-horizon capabilities that protect trade routes and international waters, and shall ask regional allies to share local defense burdens. Reduced forward presence and the end of regime-change operations are severable commitments; either may proceed without the other.

**Peace Through Strength**

Military strength exists primarily to prevent war, not seek it.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 24 Defense.

### 23.3 Diplomacy First Framework \[Statute\]

The United States shall prioritize:

- Diplomacy

- Conflict prevention

- Alliance management

- Economic cooperation

- Peaceful dispute resolution

before the use of military force.

**Diplomacy First Principle**

Military action should remain available when necessary, but policymakers should exhaust reasonable diplomatic alternatives before resorting to armed conflict.

### 23.4 Alliance & Strategic Partnership Initiative \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain and strengthen alliances with democratic partners around the world.

Priority objectives include:

- Defense cooperation

- Economic cooperation

- Scientific collaboration

- Intelligence sharing

- Joint security planning

Strong alliances reduce the likelihood of conflict and multiply collective security.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 24 Defense.

### 23.5 Strategic Foreign Assistance Program \[Statute\]

Foreign assistance should advance both humanitarian values and long-term stability.

Federal foreign-aid programs shall support three primary objectives:

#### 23.5.1 Humanitarian Stability

Programs may support:

- Disaster relief

- Food security

- Refugee assistance

- Clean water access

- Public health initiatives

The objective is reducing human suffering and preventing instability before it becomes crisis.

#### 23.5.2 Economic Development

Programs may support:

- Education

- Workforce development

- Agricultural modernization

- Infrastructure investment

- Disease prevention

Economic development reduces long-term instability and creates future trading partners.

#### 23.5.3 Democratic Resilience

The United States may support initiatives that encourage:

- Transparent governance

- Anti-corruption reforms

- Civil society development

- Independent institutions

- Rule of law

The objective is supporting stable societies rather than imposing political outcomes.

### 23.6 Accountability Standards for Foreign Assistance \[Statute\]

All major aid programs shall include:

- Performance metrics

- Independent audits

- Transparency reporting

- Outcome evaluations

Foreign assistance should be held to the same accountability standards expected of domestic programs.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 14 Government Effectiveness.

### 23.7 Friendship Through Partnership Principle

People who experience American assistance, cooperation, and goodwill are more likely to become future partners than future adversaries.

Economic cooperation, educational exchanges, scientific partnerships, and humanitarian assistance can strengthen international relationships while advancing American interests.

### 23.8 International Scientific & Educational Cooperation \[Statute\]

The United States shall encourage peaceful international collaboration involving:

- Scientific research

- Medical innovation

- Educational exchanges

- Space exploration

- Technology development

Such cooperation promotes both security and prosperity.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 18.
