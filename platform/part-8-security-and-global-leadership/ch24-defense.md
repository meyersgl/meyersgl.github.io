## Chapter 24: Defense

### 24.1 Guiding Principle

The United States shall maintain the world's most capable defensive military while ensuring responsible stewardship of public resources.

Military strength is essential to:

- Deterring aggression

- Protecting allies

- Preserving freedom of navigation

- Defending national sovereignty

Defense spending should prioritize capability and readiness rather than political favoritism.

### 24.2 Military Readiness, Modernization & Strategic Procurement Act \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Defense procurement shall emphasize:

- Military effectiveness

- Technological superiority

- Operational readiness

- Cost accountability

#### 24.2.1 Military Capability First Doctrine

Procurement decisions should prioritize measurable capability and mission effectiveness.

Weapons systems should be evaluated according to performance rather than political considerations.

#### 24.2.2 Competitive Defense Acquisition Reform

The Department of Defense shall maximize competition whenever practical.

**Competitive Procurement Standard**

Major acquisition programs should utilize competitive bidding processes.

**Sole-Source Transparency**

Contracts awarded without competition shall require public justification whenever national security permits.

#### 24.2.3 Defense Cost Overrun Accountability

Programs experiencing significant:

- Cost overruns

- Performance failures

- Schedule delays

shall automatically undergo independent review.

Potential remedies include:

- Restructuring

- Re-competition

- Program cancellation

### 24.3 Defense Industrial Base Resilience \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain domestic production capacity for strategically important defense industries.

Priority sectors include:

- Shipbuilding

- Aerospace

- Electronics

- Semiconductors

- Munitions

- Critical minerals

Cross-reference: See Chapter 21 Infrastructure, Industry & Supply Chains.

### 24.4 Rapid Technology Adoption Program \[Statute\]

The Department of Defense shall accelerate adoption of technologies including:

- Artificial intelligence

- Robotics

- Advanced manufacturing

- Cybersecurity

- Space systems

- Quantum technologies

Innovation should not become trapped in bureaucratic acquisition processes.

### 24.5 Startup & Small Contractor Competition Initiative \[Statute\]

Federal procurement shall create pathways allowing innovative small businesses to compete for defense contracts.

Programs may include:

- Prototype projects

- Demonstration programs

- Innovation grants

- Accelerated procurement channels

The objective is increasing innovation and competition.

### 24.6 Military Logistics & Readiness Modernization \[Statute\]

Readiness planning shall emphasize:

- Supply-chain resilience

- Fuel security

- Transportation capacity

- Maintenance readiness

- Cyber resilience

A modern military depends upon logistics as much as weapon systems.

### 24.7 Cyber Defense & Digital Security Program \[Statute – High Resistance\]

The federal government shall continuously strengthen military cyber capabilities.

Priority areas include:

- Network defense

- Supply-chain security

- Communications protection

- Infrastructure coordination

Cybersecurity is now a core national-defense requirement.

### 24.8 Space Security & Resilience Initiative \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Space-based systems support:

- Navigation

- Communications

- Weather forecasting

- Missile warning

- Intelligence systems

Federal policy shall ensure their resilience and continuity.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 18 Science, Research & Space.

### 24.9 Service Member Quality of Life Standard \[Statute\]

Military readiness depends upon talented personnel.

Federal policy shall prioritize:

- Housing quality

- Healthcare access

- Family support services

- Education opportunities

- Transition assistance

for service members and military families.

### 24.10 Veterans Skills Transition Program \[Statute\]

Military training and certifications shall receive recognition within civilian workforce systems whenever practical.

Cross-reference: See Section 3.5 Workforce Mobility & Occupational Licensing Reform.

### 24.11 Strategic Deterrence Principle

The purpose of military strength is preserving peace through deterrence.

Military power should defend national interests and allies while avoiding unnecessary intervention and open-ended nation-building commitments.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 23 Foreign Policy & Diplomacy.
