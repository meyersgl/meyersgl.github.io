# APPENDIX C: INSTITUTIONAL & REGULATORY DESIGN STANDARDS

## Purpose

This appendix sets the design rules that every institution and regulation in the platform follows, so that rules, enforcement, and exemptions work the same way across sectors and remain legally durable.

## Institutions After *Trump v. Slaughter*

In *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026), the Supreme Court held that Congress may not protect the heads of executive agencies from removal at will, with the Federal Reserve treated as an exception in *Trump v. Cook*. The Court left open protections for legislative-branch bodies and for inferior officers who report to accountable superiors.

The platform's rule is to **take discretion away rather than try to protect the person who exercises it**. Formulas, deadlines, and publication duties are written into statute, so an official who can be removed has little left to bend.

### Five Durability Tools

1. **Legislative-branch placement** for advisory, scoring, and audit work (the Congressional Budget Office, the Joint Committee on Taxation, the Government Accountability Office, and advisory commissions modeled on MedPAC).
2. **Formulas in statute**: rates, thresholds, and investment mandates are set in law, and agencies apply and publish them.
3. **Reasoned, public decisions**: rejections and overrides must be published with written reasons, and removals of watchdog officials require written reasons and advance notice to Congress.
4. **Protection for career staff**: civil-service and inferior-officer protections, rather than protections for agency heads.
5. **Courts and third parties**: duties enforceable in federal court, independent outside evaluators, and Inspector General audits.

| Justice Department independence | 15.11 | Contact logs, disclosure to Congress, and protections for career prosecutors |
| Sentencing review | 16.4 | The United States Sentencing Commission, in the judicial branch |
| Critical-supplies list | 21.6.1 | Recommended by a national-laboratory technical office using a published method. Issued by rule subject to congressional review |
| Commission on Civil Rights | 13.7.4 | Legislative-branch commission appointed by congressional leaders of both parties. Investigates and reports, with no enforcement power |
| Civil rights enforcement | 13.7 | Private lawsuits with attorney fees, state attorney general enforcement, a right to sue after 180 days without agency action, published reasons for abandoned cases |
| Pay data | 13.6.1 | Collected by a statistical agency under a mandatory statutory duty that any person may sue to compel |
| Rent stabilization floor | 8.2.3, 8.2.5 | Formula and coverage set in statute with no waiver authority. | Homelessness programs | 8.3.1, 8.3.4 | Permanent housing floor and renewal rule set in statute. Funding notice deadline with automatic renewal of prior terms, notice and comment for any change in criteria, a statutory Interagency Council on Homelessness, and private and state enforcement of encampment conditions |
| Officer accountability | 16.9 | One statutory cause of action for state, local, and federal officers, enforced in court with no qualified immunity. A mandatory public reporting duty that any person may sue to compel |
| Pattern-or-practice enforcement | 16.14 | Concurrent state attorney general authority and published reasons for abandoned cases or ended consent decrees |
| Frontier AI oversight | 19.6 | Commerce/NIST. Triggers and deadlines set in statute, independent evaluators, penalties enforced in court |

## Cross-Sector Regulatory Rules

| Rule | Applies to | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominance Threshold | 8.1.3, 9.2, 10.1.5 | Conduct-based rules. Extra duties apply only above one defined threshold, never based on size alone |
| Strategic Protection Test | 11.2, 21.6.1, 21.8, 24.3 | A documented need, a capped cost, a sunset date, and congressional authority for any tariff |
| Coordinated Review Standard | 8.1.2, 20.6, 21.9 | Faster permitting through staff, coordination, and timelines, never through weaker review |
| Pilot rule | 14.5 | Pilots only for untested program designs |
| Paperwork rule | 14.2.5 | One-in-one-out applies to paperwork and reporting burdens only |
| Honest limits | All High Resistance and Constitutionally Contested provisions | Each states its main legal or political risk |
