## Chapter 3: Human Infrastructure

### 3.1 Guiding Principle

A prosperous society is built not only through economic growth, but through investments in people. Healthcare, education, housing, communications infrastructure, and consumer rights form the foundation upon which individuals and families build successful lives.

The purpose of public investment is not to eliminate private markets. The purpose is to ensure every citizen has access to the essential systems required to participate fully in society and the economy.

### 3.2 Universal Health Insurance \[Statute – High Resistance\]

*Aligned with the white paper* Universal Health Insurance: A Hybrid Framework for Universal Coverage *(decision 0001).*

The United States shall establish a national public health insurance system providing universal coverage for all citizens, lawful permanent residents, and other lawfully present residents. Emergency care, treatment of communicable disease, and public-health services shall be available to all persons regardless of immigration status, consistent with the Human Dignity Standard.

The system shall have two layers:

- **Universal Health Insurance**, a publicly financed plan that provides comprehensive coverage to every eligible resident.
- **Supplemental insurance**, which individuals may purchase privately for services beyond the standard package, upgraded accommodations, or other added benefits. Supplemental insurance shall add to the public plan and shall not replace it.

Healthcare providers shall remain privately operated and independently managed. The public system shall function primarily as the financing mechanism while preserving provider independence and patient choice.

#### 3.2.1 Covered Services

Coverage shall include all medically necessary:

- Primary care
- Specialist care
- Preventive care
- Emergency care
- Hospitalization
- Surgical care
- Mental-health treatment
- Substance-use treatment
- Prescription drugs
- Dental care
- Vision care
- Hearing care
- Home health services
- Long-term care
- Fertility treatment
- Rehabilitation services
- Reproductive and maternal health, including contraception, pregnancy and postpartum care, and abortion care (Section 3.12)

Covered services shall carry no copays or deductibles at the point of care. Because removing cost-sharing also removes a common check on how much care is used, spending growth shall be managed through provider-payment design and workforce planning (Sections 3.2.3 and 3.2.5).

The long-term care benefit shall be designed after an independent actuarial study of its sustainability over multiple decades, completed before universal enrollment begins.

#### 3.2.2 Financing

Universal Health Insurance shall be financed through a dedicated Universal Health Insurance Trust Fund, funded by payroll contributions, employer contributions, and income-based taxes (Section 22.3). These contributions are intended to replace the premiums, deductibles, and out-of-pocket costs that households and employers pay today. Savings from consolidated health financing shall lower contribution rates and shall not be counted elsewhere. Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue shall not be used for health financing (Section 22.5). Contribution rates shall be set after independent scoring.

#### 3.2.3 Provider Payments and Rate Recommendations

Provider payments shall follow a national fee schedule set in statute, using Medicare's relative-value (RVU) weighting system as its foundation and adjusted for geographic cost differences. Medicare-based rates are lower than what private insurers pay hospitals today, so the move toward them shall be gradual and monitored for effects on access, closures, and the workforce.

A health-payment advisory commission in the legislative branch, modeled on the Medicare Payment Advisory Commission (MedPAC), shall recommend annual updates based on medical evidence, operational sustainability, geographic cost differences, and long-term system efficiency. A recommendation shall take effect unless the Secretary of Health and Human Services publishes a written, reasoned decision rejecting it.

#### 3.2.4 Specialty & Geographic Appeals Mechanism

A formal, data-driven appeals process shall allow specialties and regions to challenge reimbursement rates so that rates can be corrected before shortages or closures occur (for example, in oncology or rural family medicine). Appeals shall be decided by administrative law judges.

#### 3.2.5 Workforce Development

Shortages of physicians, nurses, dentists, optometrists, and behavioral-health providers shall be addressed through:

- Residency and training-capacity expansion
- Rural and underserved-area service incentives
- Loan repayment for health professionals with loans taken out before tuition-free public higher education (Section 3.7) takes effect
- Workforce development programs

Cross-reference: See Chapter 2 Workforce Mobility and Chapter 12 Science & Innovation.

#### 3.2.6 Phased Implementation

Implementation shall proceed in phases, with costs evaluated, outcomes measured, and problems corrected at each stage:

1. Expanded Medicare buy-in options. Because Medicare's Hospital Insurance trust fund is projected to run out of reserves in 2033, this phase shall carry its own financing.
2. Coverage expansion for children.
3. Gradual expansion into broader age groups.
4. Universal enrollment.

Implementing legislation shall coordinate Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' health programs, and employer-sponsored coverage so that no one loses coverage during the transition.

#### 3.2.7 Honest Limit

This framework has not yet been independently scored. Open questions include the exact financing mix and rates, how fast payments should move toward Medicare-based levels, what supplemental coverage may duplicate, how to manage use of care without cost-sharing, the design of a sustainable long-term care benefit, and how quickly the workforce can grow. These are set out for expert review in the white paper.

### 3.3 Drug Pricing & Dynamic Global Innovation Fund \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Healthcare affordability should not come at the expense of medical innovation.

#### 3.3.1 National Price Negotiation

The Universal Health Insurance system shall negotiate prescription-drug prices to obtain value for patients and taxpayers while maintaining incentives for research and development. This extends the negotiation Medicare already conducts under current law.

#### 3.3.2 Dynamic Global Innovation Fund

A Dynamic Global Innovation Fund shall support breakthrough therapies and pioneering medical discoveries. Funding shall come from a fixed statutory share of the savings from national drug-price negotiation, subject to an annual cap (Section 22.3). Only the remaining savings shall be counted in health-cost estimates.

#### 3.3.3 Variable Innovation Exclusivity

New therapies receiving approval shall qualify for a period of regulatory market exclusivity ranging from 7 to 15 years, based upon:

- Research and development expenditures
- Therapeutic benefit
- Public-health impact
- Scientific advancement
- Innovation significance

This exclusivity is a regulatory period layered on top of existing patent rights. It does not shorten patent terms, consistent with U.S. obligations under the WTO TRIPS Agreement.

#### 3.3.4 Public Patent Option and Generic Competition

When the awarded exclusivity period ends, generic competition shall be encouraged and public manufacturing rights shall become available, so that treatment costs decline.

#### 3.3.5 Manufacturing and Strategic Procurement

Pharmaceutical production shall remain primarily within the private sector. The government may contract private manufacturers to produce medicines needed to address shortages or access problems.

### 3.4 Rural Healthcare Stability Framework \[Statute\]

Healthcare access shall not depend upon population density. Rural stability programs shall be funded through the Universal Health Insurance Trust Fund (Section 22.4).

#### 3.4.1 Geographic Reimbursement Adjustments

Reimbursement rates shall account for rural operating realities using a Geographic Volatility Index.

#### 3.4.2 Rural Stabilization Grants and Operational Support

Healthcare providers facing patient-volume shortages may receive automatic stabilization payments and targeted grants designed to prevent closure of essential facilities.

Priority shall be given to:

- Rural hospitals
- Critical access hospitals
- Community clinics
- Mental-health providers

#### 3.4.3 Minimum Service Guarantees

Basic healthcare services shall remain available regardless of geographic location.

### 3.5 Universal Preschool \[Statute\]

Every four-year-old child shall have access to publicly funded preschool education.

The goals are:

- Early childhood development

- School readiness

- Workforce participation

- Reduced long-term educational inequality

### 3.6 K-12 Educational Opportunity Framework \[Statute\]

Every child shall have access to a high-quality public education regardless of zip code, local property values, or family income. Educational opportunity shall be a national commitment rather than a function of local wealth.

### 3.6.1 Equal Opportunity Funding

Federal and state education funding shall reduce disparities created by local property-tax dependence. Public schools serving low-income communities shall receive sufficient resources to provide educational opportunities substantially equivalent to those available in wealthier districts. Federal funding shall be weighted toward high-poverty districts and tied to state equity-of-funding requirements.

### 3.6.2 National Education Standards

States shall retain control over curriculum while ensuring students receive adequate instruction in mathematics, science, literacy, civics, history, technology, and critical thinking. Minimum standards in these subjects shall be developed cooperatively with the states and tied to federal funding.

### 3.7 Tuition-Free Public Higher Education \[Statute\]

Tuition shall be fully covered at:

- Public universities

- Community colleges

- Trade schools

- Technical institutions

Federal assistance currently tied to tuition costs should instead be redirected toward:

- Housing

- Food

- Books

- Transportation

- Living expenses

for qualifying students.

#### 3.7.1 Existing Student Loan Reform \[Statute\]

Existing federal student loans shall be refinanced at a near-zero administrative interest rate of 0.01%.

Borrowers making qualifying payments for twenty years (240 monthly payments) shall be eligible for full debt discharge.

Additional penalties, fees, and capitalization of unpaid interest shall be prohibited.

Cost estimates for this provision shall be published under Chapter 22 before implementation.

### 3.8 Public Community Internet \[Statute – High Resistance\]

States may not prohibit municipalities from constructing or operating public broadband infrastructure.

Communities shall retain the right to establish publicly owned broadband services whenever local residents determine such systems are necessary.

Broadband access shall be treated as essential economic infrastructure.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 16 Infrastructure & Industrial Capacity.

### 3.9 Rights-Based Data Privacy \[Statute\]

Individuals shall possess enforceable legal rights over their personal data.

Citizens shall possess the right to:

- View personal data

- Correct personal data

- Delete personal data

- Transfer personal data

without unreasonable barriers.

Corporations may not treat consumer data as a commodity or as permanently owned corporate property.

### 3.10 Right to Repair \[Statute\]

Consumers shall have the right to repair products they legally own.

Manufacturers shall provide:

- Replacement parts

- Software tools

- Diagnostic systems

- Repair manuals

on fair and reasonable terms.

Artificial repair barriers designed solely to prevent independent repair shall be prohibited.

### 3.11 Affordable Housing Initiative \[Statute / Statute – High Resistance\]

Housing affordability represents one of the largest economic challenges facing American families.

Federal policy shall focus on increasing housing supply rather than restricting development.

#### 3.11.1 Upzoning Incentives

Federal transportation and housing funding may be conditioned upon local housing production reforms.

#### 3.11.2 Permitting Modernization

Investments shall support:

- Digital permitting systems

- Staffing expansion

- Pre-approved building designs

- Clear, predictable review timelines achieved through staffing and capacity, without weakening environmental or safety standards

#### 3.11.3 Institutional Ownership Limits

Federal policy shall restrict excessive concentration of single-family housing within large institutional investment portfolios.

#### 3.11.4 Public Housing Developer

A publicly operated housing developer shall construct and maintain mixed-income housing developments in high-demand markets.

#### 3.11.5 Anti-Gouging Rent Caps

Federal incentives shall support state and local caps on annual rent increases tied to inflation, with newly constructed housing exempt for a defined period so that building is not discouraged.

#### 3.11.6 First-Time Homebuyer Assistance

Down payment assistance shall be available expressly to first-time homebuyers. It shall be income-limited and designed (for example, with shared-equity terms) so that it does not simply bid up prices, and it shall be paired with the supply measures above.

#### 3.11.7 Homeowner Protections

Property-tax relief for seniors and low-income homeowners shall ensure that rising land values do not force long-time residents out. Owners shall have a by-right ability to add accessory units or split lots. Housing reforms shall be phased in so that no homeowner's equity is wiped out.

### 3.12 Reproductive Health Protection \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Federal law shall protect access to reproductive healthcare, including contraception, abortion care, and pregnancy and postpartum care, nationwide.

Reproductive and maternal health services, including contraception, abortion care, and pregnancy and postpartum care, shall be covered under the public insurance system in Section 3.2.

#### 3.12.1 Abortion Care Is Health Care

Abortion care is women's health care. It shall be legal nationwide so that women can get the medical care they need.

The decision whether to have an abortion belongs to the woman and her doctor. Government shall not substitute its judgment for theirs, and medical decisions shall be guided by clinical standards of care, not by political interference.

#### 3.12.2 Federal Protection of Access

Federal law shall guarantee the legal right to abortion nationwide and shall preempt state laws that ban abortion or impose restrictions that are not medically necessary. Access to medication abortion, including by telehealth, shall be protected from restrictions that are not supported by medical evidence.

#### 3.12.3 Protection for Patients and Providers

Doctors and other clinicians shall be free to provide abortion care according to their medical judgment without threat of criminal or civil penalty. Patients shall not be criminalized for their pregnancy outcomes. Patients, providers, and those who assist them shall be protected from penalties imposed by another state for care that is lawful where it is provided.

#### 3.12.4 Coverage and Access

Abortion care shall be covered under the public insurance system in Section 3.2, and federal policy shall support access in areas with few providers.

#### 3.12.5 Honest Limit

Federal legislation can be repealed by a later Congress, and its constitutional basis will be challenged in court. Because this section leaves individual decisions to patients and their doctors rather than setting legislated limits, expect the sharpest opposition on later-term abortion.
