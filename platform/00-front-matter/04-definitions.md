# Definitions

**The following definitions shall apply throughout The Social Democratic Market Platform unless otherwise specified by statute, constitutional amendment, or implementing legislation.**

**Living Wage**

**A Living Wage is the minimum hourly compensation necessary for a full-time worker to meet basic living expenses within a specific geographic area without requiring public assistance for ordinary necessities.**

**The calculation shall consider:**

- **Housing**

- **Food**

- **Transportation**

- **Healthcare**

- **Childcare**

- **Utilities**

- **Basic communication services**

- **Essential savings**

**A Living Wage may vary by region to reflect differences in local costs of living.**

**The Living Wage serves as the benchmark used to set the percentage of the federal poverty line in Section 3.1.**

Costs met by universal public programs established in this platform (such as public health insurance, preschool, and capped childcare) shall be excluded from the calculation to avoid double-counting.

**Productive Capital Expenditure**

**A Productive Capital Expenditure is an investment that increases long-term economic output, productive capacity, workforce capability, infrastructure quality, or scientific advancement.**

**Examples include:**

- **Manufacturing expansion**

- **Research and development**

- **Workforce training**

- **Infrastructure construction**

- **Technology upgrades**

- **Equipment modernization**

**Productive Capital Expenditures shall be distinguished from purely speculative or consumptive expenditures.**

**Critical Emergency Supplies**

**Critical Emergency Supplies are goods necessary to maintain public safety, public health, or national resilience during emergencies.**

**These may include:**

- **Medical equipment**

- **Essential pharmaceuticals**

- **Protective equipment**

- **Emergency communications systems**

- **Water-treatment materials**

- **Energy-related emergency components**

- **Disaster-response equipment**

**The federal government may designate additional categories when justified by national risk assessments.**

**Frontier AI System**

**A Frontier AI System is an artificial intelligence model or collection of models demonstrating capabilities sufficiently advanced to create substantial economic, societal, cybersecurity, national-security, or public-safety consequences.**

**Determinations shall rest primarily on demonstrated performance, assessed under the Algorithmic Capability Index, with training compute serving as a screening trigger (Section 19.2.1).**

**Autonomous AI Action**

**An Autonomous AI Action is a decision, activity, process, or sequence of operations performed by an artificial intelligence system without direct human approval for each individual action at the time the action occurs.**

**Examples may include:**

- **Independent cybersecurity operations**

- **Resource allocation decisions**

- **Multi-step task execution**

- **Software deployment**

- **Financial transactions**

**Routine automated functions with clearly constrained authority may receive separate regulatory treatment.**

**Strategic Industry**

**A Strategic Industry is an economic sector whose disruption would significantly affect national security, economic stability, public health, or critical infrastructure.**

**Strategic industries may include:**

- **Semiconductors**

- **Pharmaceuticals**

- **Biotechnology**

- **Aerospace**

- **Energy production**

- **Advanced manufacturing**

- **Communications infrastructure**

- **Critical mineral production**

**Congress may periodically revise this designation.**

**Human Dignity Standard**

**The Human Dignity Standard requires that individuals interacting with public institutions be treated with fairness, respect, due process, and recognition of their inherent worth regardless of citizenship status, income level, race, religion, disability, age, sex, or political affiliation.**

**This standard applies throughout:**

- **Healthcare systems**

- **Criminal justice systems**

- **Immigration systems**

- **Public-benefit administration**

- **Government services**

**Addiction Hotspot**

**An Addiction Hotspot is a geographic area experiencing unusually high rates of:**

- **Substance dependency**

- **Drug overdoses**

- **Addiction-related hospitalizations**

- **Addiction-related criminal activity**

**Designation may qualify a community for targeted prevention, treatment, and recovery resources.**

**Beneficial Ownership**

**Beneficial Ownership refers to the natural person or persons who ultimately control, profit from, or possess economic interest in an asset, corporation, trust, partnership, or other legal entity, regardless of intermediary ownership structures.**

**Beneficial ownership disclosure requirements shall be used to reduce:**

- **Tax evasion**

- **Money laundering**

- **Fraud**

- **Concealed political influence**

- **Illicit financial activity**

**National Resilience**

**National Resilience is the ability of the United States to prepare for, withstand, respond to, recover from, and adapt to disruptive events while maintaining essential societal functions.**

**National resilience encompasses:**

- **Economic resilience**

- **Infrastructure resilience**

- **Disaster preparedness**

- **Public-health readiness**

- **Supply-chain security**

- **Cybersecurity**

- **Energy reliability**

- **Democratic continuity**

**Competitive Market**

**A Competitive Market is a market characterized by:**

- **Multiple sellers**

- **Consumer choice**

- **Relatively low barriers to entry**

- **Transparent information**

- **Fair competition**

**Competition should occur on the basis of:**

- **Innovation**

- **Quality**

- **Efficiency**

- **Service**

**rather than anti-competitive conduct or regulatory favoritism.**

**Family Farm**

**A Family Farm is an agricultural business primarily owned, managed, and operated by an individual, family, or family-controlled entity rather than by a publicly traded corporation, foreign government, or large institutional investment group.**

**Evidence-Based Governance**

**Evidence-Based Governance is the practice of designing, evaluating, and modifying public policy according to measurable outcomes, empirical evidence, scientific research, and transparent performance metrics rather than ideological preference alone.**

**Programs should be evaluated according to:**

- **Effectiveness**

- **Efficiency**

- **Cost-benefit performance**

- **Public outcomes**

- **Long-term sustainability**

**Public Foundation**

**The Public Foundation refers collectively to the core public systems necessary for opportunity and long-term prosperity, including:**

- **Healthcare**

- **Education**

- **Infrastructure**

- **Public safety**

- **Democratic institutions**

- **Scientific research**

- **Environmental stewardship**

- **Equal opportunity protections**

**The Public Foundation is the central organizing principle of this platform.**

**Sovereign Wealth Reserve**

**The Sovereign Wealth Reserve is a publicly owned investment fund designed to:**

- **Stabilize economic cycles**

- **Hold Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue up to a statutory stabilization target, then pass a smoothed share to the General Fund for later-tier programs (Section 27.5)**

- **Strengthen fiscal resilience**

**Assets shall be professionally managed under transparent and independent governance structures. The Reserve exists for recession stabilization. Apart from the smoothed transfer, no other program may claim it (Section 27.5).**

**Dominance Threshold**

**A firm meets the Dominance Threshold in a market when it holds a durable share of that market and is protected by barriers to entry that prevent effective competition, as specified in statute and in rules issued by the enforcing agencies. Size alone shall never trigger intervention. Duties that apply above the threshold target conduct. Used in Sections 8.1.3, 9.2, and 10.1.5.**

**Strategic Protection Test**

**A trade restriction, sourcing requirement, or domestic-production preference meets the Strategic Protection Test only if it (1) addresses a documented national-security or resilience need identified under Section 21.6, (2) has an estimated and capped cost to consumers and businesses, (3) carries a sunset and review date, and (4) in the case of a tariff, rests on authority expressly granted by Congress. Used in Sections 11.2, 21.6.1, 21.8, and 24.3.**

**Coordinated Review Standard**

**Faster permitting shall come from funded staff, coordinated agency review, and clear timelines, never from narrowing environmental or safety review or limiting public participation (Section 21.9).**

**Capital Investment and Recurring Obligation**

**A capital investment creates a long-lived productive asset, such as infrastructure or research facilities. A recurring obligation is an ongoing program cost. Recurring obligations shall be paid only from stable revenue (Chapter 27).**

**Hosted and Open-Weight Models**

**A hosted model runs on infrastructure its developer controls and can be suspended. An open-weight model's parameters have been publicly released and cannot be recalled (Chapter 19).**

**Statutory Interpretation Principle**

**When ambiguity in the platform's statutory provisions must be resolved, interpretation should favor the following Governing Principles, in this order:**

1.  **Democratic accountability.**

2.  **Human dignity.**

3.  **Competitive markets.**

4.  **Scientific evidence.**

5.  **Long-term national resilience.**

6.  **Equal opportunity.**

7.  **Fiscal responsibility.**

**The full statement of each principle appears in the Governing Principles.**

No major program in this platform shall take effect without an identified funding source and an independent cost estimate under Chapter 27.
