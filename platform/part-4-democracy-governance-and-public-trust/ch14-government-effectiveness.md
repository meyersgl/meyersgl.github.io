## Chapter 14: Government Effectiveness

*Funding role: the reviews, dashboards, anti-fraud office, and other activities in this chapter are funded within existing authorized appropriations, with no Section 27.9 phase-in obligation. One-time replacement of long-lived federal systems under Section 14.3 may use the Capital Investment Account and productive borrowing (Sections 27.5 and 27.7).*

### 14.1 Guiding Principle

Government should not be judged by its size alone.

Government should be judged by its effectiveness, transparency, accountability, and measurable results.

The purpose of public administration is to deliver services efficiently, responsibly, and in a manner that earns public trust.

Taxpayer resources are valuable and should be managed with the same discipline expected of any successful institution.

### 14.2 Taxpayer Accountability & Government Performance Act \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall be required to regularly demonstrate effectiveness, efficiency, and measurable public benefit.

Programs should be evaluated according to outcomes rather than political ideology.

#### 14.2.1 Ten-Year Performance Reviews

Every major federal program shall be reviewed by the Government Accountability Office, supported by agency Inspectors General, at least once every ten years.

Reviews shall evaluate:

- Program outcomes

- Fiscal sustainability

- Administrative efficiency

- Fraud prevention

- Public utilization

- Cost effectiveness

Findings shall be publicly available.

#### 14.2.2 Public Performance Dashboards

Federal agencies shall maintain public dashboards displaying:

- Program expenditures

- Administrative overhead

- Service delivery times

- Performance indicators

- Outcome measurements

The goal is to make government performance understandable and accessible to citizens.

#### 14.2.3 Automatic Sunset Reviews

New federal programs shall include review dates requiring formal congressional evaluation.

Programs shall not automatically terminate, but Congress must actively assess effectiveness before reauthorization.

#### 14.2.4 Regulatory Simplification Initiative

Federal agencies shall continuously identify:

- Obsolete regulations

- Duplicative rules

- Contradictory requirements

- Excessive paperwork burdens

while preserving:

- Consumer protections

- Public safety

- Labor standards

- Environmental safeguards

#### 14.2.5 One-In-One-Out Administrative Rule

When practical, agencies creating major new reporting requirements should identify existing requirements suitable for elimination or consolidation.

This rule applies only to paperwork and reporting burdens, never to substantive protections for consumers, workers, public health, safety, or the environment. The objective is administrative efficiency rather than deregulation for its own sake.

### 14.3 Digital Government Modernization \[Statute\]

Federal services should be accessible through secure and modern digital systems.

Modernization priorities include:

- Secure online filing

- Digital identity verification

- Interagency coordination

- Electronic benefit administration

- Cybersecurity upgrades

Citizens should not be forced to navigate outdated systems to access basic government services.

One-time replacement of outdated federal systems may be paid from the Capital Investment Account and productive borrowing (Sections 27.5 and 27.7) where independent costing under Section 27.10 shows a long-lived system. Operation and maintenance are funded within existing authorized appropriations.

### 14.4 Anti-Fraud Enforcement Unit \[Statute\]

A permanent anti-fraud office, funded within existing authorized appropriations, shall investigate:

- Procurement fraud

- Contractor abuse

- Improper payments

- Grant misuse

- Financial misconduct

Recovered funds shall be returned to the program they came from and are not counted as platform revenue (Section 27.3).

### 14.5 Outcome-Based Pilot Programs \[Statute\]

Major initiatives may begin as pilot programs before nationwide deployment.

Pilot programs shall be evaluated using measurable outcomes before expansion. Pilots are required only for program designs that have not yet been tested; designs with established evidence may be phased in nationally under Section 27.9.

### 14.6 Evidence-Based Governance Requirement \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall prioritize evidence, data, and measurable outcomes when developing policy.

Whenever feasible, agencies shall utilize:

- Pilot programs

- Independent evaluations

- State-level demonstrations

- Controlled testing

- Long-term outcome assessment

before implementing major nationwide reforms.

**Evidence Principle**

Good ideas should survive scrutiny.

Bad ideas should be revised regardless of political origin.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 18 Science, Research & Space.

### 14.7 Government Procurement Reform \[Statute\]

Federal procurement shall prioritize:

- Competition

- Transparency

- Cost efficiency

- Long-term value

**Competitive Bidding Standard**

Contracts shall utilize competitive bidding whenever practical.

**Contractor Accountability**

Contractors repeatedly responsible for:

- Fraud

- Chronic cost overruns

- Performance failures

may be suspended from future contracting.

**Cost-Benefit Transparency**

Major projects shall publicly disclose:

- Estimated costs

- Actual costs

- Project timelines

- Performance metrics

### 14.8 Honest Limits

- **The ten-year review is the most durable part of this chapter.** The Government Accountability Office is a legislative-branch agency, so its reviews under Section 14.2.1 do not depend on the President. After *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026), the heads of executive agencies, including whoever runs the anti-fraud office, can be removed at will (Appendix C), and Inspectors General can be removed with written reasons and notice to Congress (Section 15.6).
- **Reviews and dashboards create duties to report, not duties to act.** Sunset reviews ask Congress to assess programs, but one Congress cannot bind the next, and nothing happens if a review is ignored.
- **This chapter has no private enforcement.** Nobody can go to court to compel a dashboard or a review; publication duties are its main safeguard.
- **Performance measures can be gamed.** Agencies may favor metrics that are easy to improve over outcomes that matter, and the one-in-one-out rule may pressure agencies to drop useful data collection along with redundant paperwork.
- **Large IT projects often overrun.** The Government Accountability Office has long listed federal IT acquisition as a high-risk area. One-time replacement paid through the Capital Investment Account must also wait until the Sovereign Wealth Reserve reaches its target (Section 27.5).
- **No new money.** The anti-fraud office and dashboards operate within existing appropriations (decision 0042), so their scale depends on annual appropriations.
