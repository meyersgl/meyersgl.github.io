## Chapter 15: Ethics & Anti-Corruption

### 15.1 Guiding Principle

Public office is a public trust.

Officeholders should serve the public interest rather than private financial interests.

Transparency and accountability are necessary foundations of democratic government.

### 15.2 Public Integrity & Democratic Accountability Act \[Statute\]

The federal government shall establish ethics standards designed to prevent corruption, conflicts of interest, and misuse of public office.

#### 15.2.1 Comprehensive Financial Transparency

Federal elected officials and senior executive officers shall publicly disclose:

- Financial assets

- Significant liabilities

- Outside income

- Business interests

- Major gifts above established thresholds

Disclosures shall be searchable and publicly accessible.

#### 15.2.2 Expanded Blind Trust Requirements

Covered officials shall place qualifying investment assets into independently managed blind trusts.

Covered positions include:

- President

- Vice President

- Cabinet officials

- Agency heads

- Members of Congress

#### 15.2.3 Family Anti-Circumvention Rule

Officials may not evade disclosure requirements through transfers to:

- Immediate family members

- Family trusts

- Controlled entities

for the purpose of concealing financial interests.

#### 15.2.4 Stock Trading Ban & Insider Trading Penalties

Members of Congress shall be prohibited from trading individual stocks.

Criminal penalties shall apply to insider trading by government officials.

### 15.3 Lobbying & Revolving Door Reform \[Statute\]

**Cooling-Off Period**

Senior federal officials shall be prohibited from lobbying Congress or federal agencies for a defined post-service period.

**Regulatory Capture Prevention**

Senior regulators shall be restricted from accepting employment with organizations they directly regulated during their final years of service.

### 15.4 Federal Contracting Integrity Standards \[Statute\]

Major federal contractors shall disclose:

- Beneficial ownership

- Major subcontractors

- Significant political contributions

- Prior fraud findings

Repeat offenders may face suspension or debarment.

### 15.5 Whistleblower Protection Expansion \[Statute\]

Individuals reporting:

- Waste

- Fraud

- Corruption

- Abuse of authority

- Ethical violations

shall receive strong legal protections.

**Independent Reporting Channels**

Protected reporting pathways shall exist outside normal chains of command.

### 15.6 Inspector General Independence \[Statute\]

Independent Inspectors General shall maintain authority to investigate:

- Political appointees

- Agency leadership

- Procurement misconduct

- Ethics violations

**Removal Transparency**

Removal of an Inspector General shall require written justification to Congress.

### 15.7 Foreign Influence Safeguards \[Statute\]

Federal officeholders may not receive:

- Compensation

- Consulting income

- Gifts

- Financial benefits

from foreign governments while serving in office.

### 15.8 Anti-Nepotism Standards \[Statute\]

Senior officials shall not appoint immediate family members to positions over which they exercise direct authority.

Hiring shall be based upon merit and qualifications.

### 15.9 Public Ethics Commission \[Statute – High Resistance\]

An independent bipartisan commission shall:

- Conduct ethics investigations

- Issue advisory opinions

- Enforce disclosure standards

- Publish annual reports

The commission shall operate independently of direct partisan control.

### 15.10 Citizen Transparency Portal \[Statute\]

A public portal shall combine:

- Ethics disclosures

- Campaign finance reports

- Lobbying disclosures

- Procurement information

- Performance dashboards

allowing citizens to monitor government activity through a single platform.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 12 Elections & Democratic Reform and Chapter 14 Government Effectiveness.

### 15.11 Department of Justice Independence \[Statute – High Resistance\]

The Department of Justice shall conduct criminal investigations and prosecutorial decisions independently of political direction from the President, Vice President, or political campaign organizations.

Contacts between the White House and the Department regarding specific investigations or prosecutions shall be logged and disclosed to Congress, and career prosecutors shall be protected from retaliation for declining improper direction.

Honest limit: courts may hold that the Constitution gives the President control over federal prosecutions. If so, the logging, disclosure, and anti-retaliation provisions are the parts most likely to survive.

### 15.12 Emergency Powers Reform Act \[Statute\]

National emergencies shall automatically expire after a fixed period unless renewed by joint resolution of Congress.

Congress may also end an emergency early by joint resolution, which is presented to the President in the ordinary way.

Emergency authorities shall be limited to powers directly related to the underlying emergency.

### 15.13 Presidential Clemency Transparency \[Statute\]

The President shall retain constitutional pardon authority.

All pardons and commutations shall be accompanied by public disclosure of supporting recommendations and justification.

An independent review board may issue nonbinding public assessments regarding potential conflicts of interest, corruption concerns, or self-dealing.
