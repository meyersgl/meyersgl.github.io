## Chapter 12: Elections & Democratic Reform

### 12.1 Democratic Governance Principle

Every eligible citizen should be able to vote.

Every lawful vote should be counted.

Every election result should be transparent, secure, and trusted.

### 12.2 Automatic Voter Registration \[Statute\]

Citizens shall be automatically registered upon:

- Reaching voting age

- Obtaining driver's licenses

- Updating government records

while retaining the right to opt out.

### 12.3 Election Day Accessibility \[Statute\]

Election Day shall be designated a federal holiday.

Workers shall receive paid time to vote.

### 12.4 Ranked-Choice Voting & Multi-Member Districts \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Congressional districts shall be consolidated into regional multi-member districts elected through the Single Transferable Vote (STV) form of ranked-choice voting. This requires repeal of the Uniform Congressional District Act of 1967.

Objectives:

- Reduce gerrymandering

- Expand voter choice

- Improve representation

Multi-member districts substantially reduce, but do not eliminate, the payoff from gerrymandering, because regional boundaries must still be drawn. Final multi-winner results shall be tabulated and audited within a legally defined short window (for example, one week).

### 12.5 Judicial Term Limits \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Federal judges shall serve standardized eighteen-year terms through a staggered implementation process. Sitting judges shall transition to senior status in order of seniority, with the longest-serving judges first, over a phased period. This avoids abruptly stripping any sitting judge's tenure and prevents the timing of enactment from creating partisan windfalls.

### 12.6 Age Limit of 68 \[Constitutional Amendment\]

No person shall run for or hold federal elected office after reaching age 68, and federal judges shall retire at age 68. For judges, service ends at whichever comes first: eighteen years under Section 12.5 or age 68.

The objective is not medical assessment.

The objective is:

- Leadership renewal

- Generational representation

- Long-term institutional vitality

### 12.7 Campaign Finance Reform \[Statute\]

Real-time campaign-finance disclosure shall be required.

A constitutional \[Amendment\] shall be pursued to overturn Citizens United v. FEC and permit limits on independent political spending by Super PACs, drafted narrowly to protect the press and the associational rights of unions and nonprofit organizations. This is a long-term goal severable from the statutory provisions above.

The congressional stock-trading ban and other ethics protections appear in Chapter 15.

### 12.8 Election Integrity & Public Trust Act \[Statute\]

The legitimacy of democratic government depends upon public confidence that elections are secure, accurate, transparent, and fairly administered.

Federal election policy shall strengthen both election security and voter confidence through verifiable voting systems, independent auditing procedures, cybersecurity protections, and safeguards for election officials.

The objective is to ensure that every lawful vote is accurately counted, every election is transparently administered, and every result can be independently verified.

#### 12.8.1 Voter-Verifiable Paper Ballots

All federal elections shall maintain voter-verifiable paper records.

#### 12.8.2 Risk-Limiting Audits

Independent audits shall occur before certification.

#### 12.8.3 Election Cybersecurity

Federal cybersecurity support shall strengthen election infrastructure.

#### 12.8.4 Election Worker Protections

Election officials shall receive protections against intimidation and harassment.

#### 12.8.5 Universal Voter Identification Access

States requiring voter identification must provide:

- Free voter identification

- Convenient access

- Free replacement documents

#### 12.8.6 Anti-Poll Tax Principle

No citizen shall be required to spend personal funds in order to exercise the right to vote.
