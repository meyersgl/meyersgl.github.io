## Chapter 7: Government Efficiency & Program Accountability

### 7.1 Guiding Principle

Government should not be judged by its size alone.

Government should be judged by its effectiveness, transparency, accountability, and measurable results.

The purpose of public administration is to deliver services efficiently, responsibly, and in a manner that earns public trust.

Taxpayer resources are valuable and should be managed with the same discipline expected of any successful institution.

### 7.2 Taxpayer Accountability & Government Performance Act \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall be required to regularly demonstrate effectiveness, efficiency, and measurable public benefit.

Programs should be evaluated according to outcomes rather than political ideology.

#### 7.2.1 Ten-Year Performance Reviews

Every major federal program shall undergo an independent review at least once every ten years.

Reviews shall evaluate:

- Program outcomes

- Fiscal sustainability

- Administrative efficiency

- Fraud prevention

- Public utilization

- Cost effectiveness

Findings shall be publicly available.

#### 7.2.2 Public Performance Dashboards

Federal agencies shall maintain public dashboards displaying:

- Program expenditures

- Administrative overhead

- Service delivery times

- Performance indicators

- Outcome measurements

The goal is to make government performance understandable and accessible to citizens.

#### 7.2.3 Automatic Sunset Reviews

New federal programs shall include review dates requiring formal congressional evaluation.

Programs shall not automatically terminate, but Congress must actively assess effectiveness before reauthorization.

#### 7.2.4 Regulatory Simplification Initiative

Federal agencies shall continuously identify:

- Obsolete regulations

- Duplicative rules

- Contradictory requirements

- Excessive paperwork burdens

while preserving:

- Consumer protections

- Public safety

- Labor standards

- Environmental safeguards

#### 7.2.5 One-In-One-Out Administrative Rule

When practical, agencies creating major new reporting requirements should identify existing requirements suitable for elimination or consolidation.

The objective is administrative efficiency rather than deregulation for its own sake.

### 7.3 Digital Government Modernization \[Statute\]

Federal services should be accessible through secure and modern digital systems.

Modernization priorities include:

- Secure online filing

- Digital identity verification

- Interagency coordination

- Electronic benefit administration

- Cybersecurity upgrades

Citizens should not be forced to navigate outdated systems to access basic government services.

### 7.4 Anti-Fraud Enforcement Unit \[Statute\]

A permanently funded anti-fraud office shall investigate:

- Procurement fraud

- Contractor abuse

- Improper payments

- Grant misuse

- Financial misconduct

Recovered funds shall be returned to taxpayers or the originating program.

### 7.5 Outcome-Based Pilot Programs \[Statute\]

Major initiatives may begin as pilot programs before nationwide deployment.

Pilot programs shall be evaluated using measurable outcomes before expansion.

### 7.6 Evidence-Based Governance Requirement \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall prioritize evidence, data, and measurable outcomes when developing policy.

Whenever feasible, agencies shall utilize:

- Pilot programs

- Independent evaluations

- State-level demonstrations

- Controlled testing

- Long-term outcome assessment

before implementing major nationwide reforms.

**Evidence Principle**

Good ideas should survive scrutiny.

Bad ideas should be revised regardless of political origin.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 12 Science, Research & Innovation.

### 7.7 Government Procurement Reform \[Statute\]

Federal procurement shall prioritize:

- Competition

- Transparency

- Cost efficiency

- Long-term value

**Competitive Bidding Standard**

Contracts shall utilize competitive bidding whenever practical.

**Contractor Accountability**

Contractors repeatedly responsible for:

- Fraud

- Chronic cost overruns

- Performance failures

may be suspended from future contracting.

**Cost-Benefit Transparency**

Major projects shall publicly disclose:

- Estimated costs

- Actual costs

- Project timelines

- Performance metrics
