## Chapter 14: Technology & AI Safety

### 14.1 Guiding Principle

Artificial intelligence possesses enormous potential to improve human welfare.

It also introduces novel risks.

Public policy should encourage beneficial innovation while establishing safeguards against catastrophic misuse.

### 14.2 Frontier AI Safety & Containment Act \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Advanced AI systems capable of significant autonomous action shall be subject to enhanced oversight.

#### 14.2.1 Sandbox Rule

Potentially dangerous capabilities shall be tested only within isolated controlled environments.

Independent evaluation may be required before deployment.

#### 14.2.2 Incident Reporting Requirement

Developers shall promptly report incidents involving:

- Unexpected autonomy

- Containment failures

- Security breaches

- Significant safety concerns

Transparency improves public safety and accountability.

#### 14.2.3 Kill-Switch Requirement

Deployed systems shall maintain technical mechanisms capable of suspending operation during emergencies.

The objective is preserving human control over advanced systems.

#### 14.2.4 Whistleblower Protections

Individuals exposing significant AI safety failures shall receive legal protection against retaliation.

### 14.3 Algorithmic Capability Index (ACI) \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Regulation shall focus on demonstrated capability rather than hardware measurements alone.

Models shall be evaluated according to performance in areas including:

- Autonomous cybersecurity activity

- Self-replication attempts

- Financial management autonomy

- Real-world task delegation

Systems exceeding designated thresholds shall enter enhanced oversight categories.

#### 14.3.1 Rolling Review Process

Capability thresholds shall be periodically updated to reflect technological advancement.

A static regulatory framework cannot effectively govern a rapidly evolving field.

### 14.4 Stochastic Audit Rule \[Statute\]

Regulators may conduct independent testing of deployed systems through randomized auditing methods.

The objective is identifying dangerous behavior that may not appear during formal evaluations.

### 14.5 Developer Accountability for Harm \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Developers shall bear responsibility for measurable harms caused by autonomous operation occurring outside intended safety boundaries.

Liability frameworks should encourage responsible development and deployment practices.

### 14.6 Human Control Principle

Artificial intelligence should augment human decision-making, not replace democratic accountability or human responsibility.

Humans must remain ultimately responsible for consequential decisions affecting:

- Rights

- Liberty

- Safety

- Governance
