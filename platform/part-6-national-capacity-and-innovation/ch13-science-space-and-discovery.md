## Chapter 13: Science, Space & Discovery

### 13.1 Guiding Principle

The expansion of human knowledge is a public good.

Space exploration advances:

- Science

- Technology

- Education

- Economic development

- National security

Exploration is both a practical endeavor and a long-term investment in humanity's future.

### 13.2 Science, Space & Discovery Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall maintain leadership in:

- Space research

- Space technology

- Planetary science

- Human exploration

- Commercial space development

#### 13.2.1 National Space Leadership

The nation shall continue investing in:

- Deep-space research

- Space science

- Earth observation

- Communications infrastructure

- Exploration technologies

#### 13.2.2 NASA-Space Force Research Partnership

NASA, the United States Space Force, universities, national laboratories, and private industry shall cooperate on research initiatives that expand scientific understanding and technological capability.

NASA shall remain the nation's primary civilian scientific agency.

Space Force participation shall focus on:

- Infrastructure resilience

- Navigation systems

- Communications security

- Space-domain awareness

#### 13.2.3 Public-Private Space Development

Federal policy shall encourage partnerships between:

- NASA

- Universities

- Startups

- Aerospace firms

- Research institutions

to accelerate discovery and innovation.

#### 13.2.4 Long-Term Exploration Goals

National exploration goals may include:

- Lunar research facilities

- Deep-space scientific missions

- Human exploration programs

- Planetary research initiatives

The objective is expanding human knowledge and technological capability.

### 13.3 Scientific Knowledge Principle

Economic development is important.

Scientific understanding is equally important.

Human civilization benefits when knowledge expands, even when immediate commercial benefit is not obvious.
