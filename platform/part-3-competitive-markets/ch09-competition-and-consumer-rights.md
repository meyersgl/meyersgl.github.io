## Chapter 9: Competition & Consumer Rights

### 9.1 Guiding Principle

Markets function best when competition exists.

The objective of antitrust policy is not to punish successful companies.

The objective is to preserve:

- Competition

- Consumer choice

- Market entry

- Entrepreneurship

Economic power should be earned through innovation rather than protected through market dominance.

### 9.2 Competitive Markets & Anti-Monopoly Act \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Federal competition policy shall focus on anti-competitive conduct rather than company size alone.

#### 9.2.1 Conduct-Based Principle & Dominance Threshold

Corporate scale alone shall not trigger government intervention.

Government action shall focus on:

- Market abuse

- Exclusionary practices

- Barriers to entry

- Anti-competitive behavior

The additional duties in Sections 9.2.2, 9.2.3, and 9.2.5 shall apply only to firms that meet the Dominance Threshold (see Definitions).

#### 9.2.2 Anti-Self-Preferencing Rule

Digital platforms that meet the Dominance Threshold may not unfairly prioritize their own products or services above competitors utilizing the same platform.

Examples include:

- Search engines

- App stores

- Online marketplaces

- Advertising platforms

#### 9.2.3 Interoperability Requirements

Platforms that meet the Dominance Threshold shall provide reasonable mechanisms allowing consumers to:

- Transfer data

- Switch services

- Communicate across systems

while protecting privacy and security.

#### 9.2.4 Anti-Killer Acquisition Rule

Regulators may block acquisitions primarily intended to eliminate emerging competitors before meaningful competition develops.

#### 9.2.5 Open Standards Requirement

Regulators may require interoperability standards when a firm that meets the Dominance Threshold creates excessive consumer lock-in.

#### 9.2.6 Small Business Competition Office

A dedicated office shall monitor barriers facing:

- Small businesses

- Start-ups

- Independent contractors

- Entrepreneurs

and provide annual recommendations to Congress.

### 9.3 Rights-Based Data Privacy \[Statute\]

Individuals shall possess enforceable legal rights over their personal data.

Citizens shall possess the right to:

- View personal data

- Correct personal data

- Delete personal data

- Transfer personal data

without unreasonable barriers.

Corporations may not treat consumer data as a commodity or as permanently owned corporate property.

### 9.4 Right to Repair \[Statute\]

Consumers shall have the right to repair products they legally own.

Manufacturers shall provide:

- Replacement parts

- Software tools

- Diagnostic systems

- Repair manuals

on fair and reasonable terms.

Artificial repair barriers designed solely to prevent independent repair shall be prohibited.
