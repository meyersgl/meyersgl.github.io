## Chapter 4: Market Competition & Economic Opportunity

### 4.1 Guiding Principle

Markets function best when competition exists.

The objective of antitrust policy is not to punish successful companies.

The objective is to preserve:

- Competition

- Consumer choice

- Market entry

- Entrepreneurship

Economic power should be earned through innovation rather than protected through market dominance.

### 4.2 Competitive Markets & Anti-Monopoly Act \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Federal competition policy shall focus on anti-competitive conduct rather than company size alone.

#### 4.2.1 Size-Neutral Principle

Corporate scale shall not automatically trigger government intervention.

Government action shall focus on:

- Market abuse

- Exclusionary practices

- Barriers to entry

- Anti-competitive behavior

#### 4.2.2 Anti-Self-Preferencing Rule

Digital gatekeepers may not unfairly prioritize their own products or services above competitors utilizing the same platform.

Examples include:

- Search engines

- App stores

- Online marketplaces

- Advertising platforms

#### 4.2.3 Interoperability Requirements

Dominant platforms shall provide reasonable mechanisms allowing consumers to:

- Transfer data

- Switch services

- Communicate across systems

while protecting privacy and security.

#### 4.2.4 Anti-Killer Acquisition Rule

Regulators may block acquisitions primarily intended to eliminate emerging competitors before meaningful competition develops.

#### 4.2.5 Open Standards Requirement

Regulators may require interoperability standards when market concentration creates excessive consumer lock-in.

#### 4.2.6 Small Business Competition Office

A dedicated office shall monitor barriers facing:

- Small businesses

- Start-ups

- Independent contractors

- Entrepreneurs

and provide annual recommendations to Congress.

### 4.3 Agricultural Market Fairness & Family Farm Protection \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Independent family farms remain essential to:

- Food security

- Rural prosperity

- Property ownership

- Community stability

Federal policy shall promote their long-term viability.

#### 4.3.1 Protecting Family Farms

Priority federal support shall be provided through:

- Succession planning programs

- Beginning farmer programs

- Conservation incentives

- Agricultural lending initiatives

#### 4.3.2 Agricultural Competition Framework

Enhanced antitrust enforcement shall focus on:

- Meat processing

- Seed production

- Fertilizer manufacturing

- Agricultural chemicals

- Grain purchasing networks

#### 4.3.3 Fair Contract Standards

Large agricultural corporations may not engage in:

- Retaliatory contracting

- Coercive exclusivity agreements

- Unjustified discriminatory pricing

against independent producers.

#### 4.3.4 Producer Cooperative Protections

Farmers shall retain the right to organize producer cooperatives and collective bargaining associations.

#### 4.3.5 Agricultural Land Protection

Large acquisitions of agricultural land by:

- Foreign governments

- Foreign state-owned entities

- Dominant institutional investment groups

shall receive enhanced federal review.

#### 4.3.6 Working Lands Conservation Partnership \[Statute\]

Conservation efforts should support working farms rather than displace them.

Programs may include:

- Soil-health incentives

- Watershed protection

- Regenerative agriculture

- Carbon sequestration programs

- Wildlife habitat initiatives

Participation shall remain voluntary.

#### 4.3.7 Nutrition-Oriented Agricultural Support

Federal agricultural subsidies shall prioritize production of foods that contribute to a healthy and affordable national diet. Changes shall be phased in over a multi-year transition so that farmers can adjust crops and contracts.

Priority categories may include:

- Fruits

- Vegetables

- Legumes

- Dairy

- Protein production

- Sustainable soil-health practices

#### 4.3.8 National Farm Risk Protection Program

Family farms shall have access to comprehensive crop and disaster insurance protection. This program shall build on and expand the existing federal crop insurance system.

Coverage shall include:

- Drought

- Flooding

- Severe weather

- Disease outbreaks

- Market-disruption emergencies

Premium assistance shall prioritize family farms and independent producers.
