## Chapter 5: Trade, Economic Partnership & Industrial Resilience

### 5.1 Guiding Principle

Trade is a tool for prosperity, not an ideology.

The United States should pursue:

- Free trade where beneficial

- Fair trade where necessary

- Strategic protections where justified

Trade policy should strengthen both economic growth and national resilience.

### 5.2 Fair Trade & Economic Partnership Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall pursue mutually beneficial trade relationships with allies and trusted partners.

#### 5.2.1 Allied Trade Framework

Trade agreements should encourage:

- Democratic governance

- Labor standards

- Environmental standards

- Intellectual-property protections

- Fair competition

#### 5.2.2 Strategic Tariff Authority

Targeted tariffs may be deployed against foreign governments engaging in:

- Intellectual property theft

- Currency manipulation

- Forced technology transfer

- State-supported dumping

- Trade discrimination

#### 5.2.3 Supply Chain Protection Standard

Trade restrictions shall be designed to minimize unnecessary disruptions to American businesses dependent upon global supply chains.

Policies must carefully balance:

- National security

- Consumer costs

- Manufacturing competitiveness

#### 5.2.4 Strategic Industry Protection

Special consideration may be given to:

- Semiconductors

- Pharmaceuticals

- Critical minerals

- Energy infrastructure

- Defense technologies

Priority sectors are defined in Section 16.4; this section governs trade treatment only.
