## Chapter 11: Trade & Strategic Protection

### 11.1 Guiding Principle

Trade is a tool for prosperity, not an ideology.

The United States should pursue:

- Free trade where beneficial

- Fair trade where necessary

- Strategic protections where justified

Trade policy should strengthen both economic growth and national resilience.

### 11.2 Fair Trade & Economic Partnership Act \[Statute\]

The United States shall pursue mutually beneficial trade relationships with allies and trusted partners.

#### 11.2.1 Allied Trade Framework

Trade agreements should encourage:

- Democratic governance

- Labor standards

- Environmental standards

- Intellectual-property protections

- Fair competition

#### 11.2.2 Strategic Tariff Authority

Targeted tariffs may be imposed against foreign governments engaging in:

- Intellectual property theft

- Currency manipulation

- Forced technology transfer

- State-supported dumping

- Trade discrimination

Tariffs under this section shall be imposed only under authority expressly granted by Congress, subject to congressional review, and shall meet the Strategic Protection Test (see Definitions). The Supreme Court held in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* (2026) that emergency-powers law does not authorize tariffs; this section makes the authority explicit and accountable.

#### 11.2.3 Supply Chain Protection Standard

Trade restrictions shall be designed to minimize unnecessary disruptions to American businesses dependent upon global supply chains.

Policies must carefully balance:

- National security

- Consumer costs

- Manufacturing competitiveness

#### 11.2.4 Strategic Industry Protection

Special consideration may be given to:

- Semiconductors

- Pharmaceuticals

- Critical minerals

- Energy infrastructure

- Defense technologies

Priority sectors are defined in Section 21.5; this section governs trade treatment only. Any protection shall meet the Strategic Protection Test.
