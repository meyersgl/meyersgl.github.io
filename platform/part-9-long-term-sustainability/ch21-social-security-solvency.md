## Chapter 21: Social Security Solvency

### 21.1 Guiding Principle

Social Security is one of the most successful anti-poverty programs in American history.

Its long-term solvency should be secured without:

- Raising the retirement age

- Reducing earned benefits

- Shifting excessive costs onto future generations

Retirement security is a foundational component of economic stability.

### 21.2 Social Security Solvency Overhaul \[Statute\]

The federal government shall strengthen Social Security through targeted revenue reforms and long-term investment strategies.

The purpose is ensuring sustainable benefits for future retirees while preserving commitments made to current beneficiaries.

#### 21.2.1 Payroll Tax Cap Reform

The payroll-tax cap shall be substantially reformed.

Income above designated thresholds shall continue contributing to Social Security financing.

The objective is ensuring that high earners contribute proportionately to system sustainability.

#### 21.2.2 Protected Middle-Income Structure

Any reforms shall be designed to avoid disproportionate burdens on:

- Middle-income workers

- Small-business employees

- Working families

The focus of financing reforms shall remain concentrated among the highest earners.

#### 21.2.3 Non-Accumulation Rule

Additional contributions above upper-income thresholds shall strengthen trust-fund finances and minimum benefits.

These additional payments shall not automatically generate corresponding increases in future high-income retirement benefits.

The objective is long-term stability rather than expanded high-income payouts.

### 21.3 Pass-Through & Business Income Reform \[Statute\]

High-income individuals should not be able to avoid Social Security contributions through accounting structures.

**Pass-Through Net**

Covered business distributions from:

- Partnerships

- S-Corporations

- Sole Proprietorships

shall remain subject to contribution requirements above designated thresholds.

**Anti-Sheltering Rule**

Corporate structures designed primarily to avoid Social Security obligations shall face corrective taxation.

### 21.4 Sovereign Endowment Pivot \[Statute\]

A supplemental Social Security Endowment shall be funded only from revenue raised by the reforms in Sections 21.2 and 21.3 that exceeds what is needed to pay scheduled benefits and maintain required trust-fund reserves. If no such excess exists, the Endowment shall not be funded. Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue shall not be used for the Endowment (Section 22.3).

These assets shall be managed independently and invested in diversified, passive, non-voting investment vehicles.

**Long-Term Compound Growth Principle**

The purpose of investment is to increase long-term trust-fund resilience while avoiding government control over private companies.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 1 Macroeconomics & Fiscal Architecture.

### 21.5 Retirement Security Principle

Economic dignity should not end when work ends.

Citizens who contribute throughout their lives should have confidence that Social Security will remain stable, reliable, and available for future generations.
