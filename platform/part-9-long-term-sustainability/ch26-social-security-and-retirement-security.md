## Chapter 26: Social Security & Retirement Security

*Aligned with the white paper* Social Security Solvency: A Proposal for Long-Term Retirement Security *(decisions 0001, 0009, 0010).*

### 26.1 Guiding Principle

Social Security is one of the most successful anti-poverty programs in American history.

Its long-term solvency should be secured without:

- Raising the retirement age

- Reducing earned benefits

- Shifting excessive costs onto future generations

Retirement security is a foundational component of economic stability. Social Security's purpose is to keep retired Americans out of poverty, not to replace very high earnings.

### 26.2 Social Security Solvency Overhaul \[Statute\]

The federal government shall strengthen Social Security through revenue reforms rather than benefit reductions.

The purpose is ensuring sustainable benefits for future retirees while preserving commitments made to current beneficiaries.

#### 26.2.1 Elimination of the Payroll Tax Cap

The Social Security payroll tax cap shall be eliminated, so that all covered earnings are subject to contributions rather than only earnings up to the taxable maximum. Medicare's Hospital Insurance payroll tax already applies to all earnings, providing a precedent. Treatment of the employer's share shall be specified in implementing legislation.

#### 26.2.2 No Additional Benefits Above the Taxable Maximum

Earnings above the taxable maximum shall not increase Social Security benefits. Benefits shall continue to be calculated only on earnings up to the taxable maximum, which shall continue to rise each year with average wage growth as under current law.

This is a deliberate financing choice: contributions above the maximum support a program whose purpose is preventing poverty in old age, without adding future benefit obligations. Economists and policymakers may reasonably disagree about whether those contributions are best described as social-insurance financing, progressive taxation, or both.

#### 26.2.3 Protected Middle-Income Structure

No payroll-tax rate increase shall apply to earnings at or below the taxable maximum. Additional financing shall come from earnings above it, protecting:

- Middle-income workers

- Small-business employees

- Working families

### 26.3 Pass-Through & Business Income Reform \[Statute\]

High-income individuals should not be able to avoid Social Security contributions through accounting structures.

**Pass-Through Net**

Business income from:

- Partnerships

- S-Corporations

- Sole Proprietorships

shall be subject to contributions on the same basis as wages, regardless of legal structure.

**Anti-Sheltering Rule**

Structures designed primarily to avoid Social Security contributions shall face corrective taxation.

### 26.4 Social Security Endowment \[Statute\]

A supplemental Social Security Endowment shall be funded only from revenue raised by the reforms in Sections 26.2 and 26.3 that exceeds what is needed to pay scheduled benefits and maintain required trust-fund reserves. If no such excess exists, the Endowment shall not be funded. Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue shall not be used for the Endowment (Section 27.3).

The Endowment shall supplement, not replace, payroll financing, and shall operate under strict rules:

- **Independent management:** investment decisions insulated from routine political pressure through a passive mandate fixed in statute.
- **Broad index investing:** assets held in diversified market index funds rather than politically directed investments.
- **Non-voting ownership:** the government shall not acquire control of private companies through Endowment holdings.
- **Transparency:** annual reports, performance and cost disclosures, and independent audits.

Because the trust funds now hold only special Treasury securities, investing Endowment assets in market index funds requires authorizing legislation.

**Long-Term Compound Growth Principle**

The purpose of investment is to increase long-term trust-fund resilience while avoiding government control over private companies.

### 26.5 Retirement Age and Means Testing \[Statute\]

The full retirement age shall remain at its current level of 67, with no further increase. Gains in life expectancy have not been shared equally, and workers in physically demanding occupations often cannot extend their careers.

No new means test shall reduce benefits based on income or wealth. Social Security already replaces a larger share of earnings for lower earners, and higher-income retirees pay income tax on up to 85 percent of their benefits. Every covered worker shall continue to contribute and to receive a benefit.

### 26.6 Honest Limit

These reforms have not yet been scored as a package by Social Security's Office of the Chief Actuary. The Chief Actuary has scored the design in Section 26.2.1, all earnings taxed at 12.4 percent with no added benefit credit, as improving the 75-year balance by about 2.55 percent of taxable payroll \[2\]. Against the 2026 Trustees Report's deficit of 4.42 percent \[1\], that closes roughly 58 percent of the gap and leaves about 1.9 percent. Section 26.3 adds revenue that has not been scored, and until the gap closes no revenue would reach the Endowment in Section 26.4 (Appendix E.4). This platform therefore does not claim full solvency until the reforms are scored. How to close any remaining gap without benefit cuts or a higher retirement age is presented as an open question for economists and actuaries in the white paper.

### 26.7 Retirement Security Principle

Economic dignity should not end when work ends.

Citizens who contribute throughout their lives should have confidence that Social Security will remain stable, reliable, and available for future generations.
