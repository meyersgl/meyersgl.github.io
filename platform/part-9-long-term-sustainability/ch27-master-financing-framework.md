## Chapter 27: Master Financing Framework

*This chapter is the single authority for every revenue and spending rule in the platform. Decision records: 0004–0008.*

### 27.1 Guiding Principle

A platform is only as credible as its funding. Every major commitment in this document shall have an identified source of revenue, an independent cost estimate, and a realistic implementation sequence.

Two rules follow. Each source of revenue shall be assigned to exactly one use, so that no dollar is promised twice. Recurring obligations shall be paid only from stable revenue; revenue that swings with markets or shrinks over time shall pay only for reserves, one-time investments, or time-limited programs.

### 27.2 Funding Source Requirement \[Statute\]

Before any major new program in this platform takes effect, an independent cost estimate and its assigned revenue stream under Section 27.3 shall be published. Programs shall be sequenced so that new obligations do not outpace identified revenue.

### 27.5 Sovereign Wealth Reserve \[Statute\]

The Sovereign Wealth Reserve (Section 1.3) shall exist only to stabilize the economy in recessions.

- **Funding:** all Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue shall be deposited in the Reserve until its stabilization balance reaches a target fixed in statute as a share of GDP.
- **Smoothed transfer:** in each year that the balance is at its target, the General Fund shall receive one-half of the lowest annual Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax receipts in the five preceding years (or in every preceding year, if fewer than five), but never more than that year's receipts. The transfer may pay only for programs in tiers 3 and 4 of Section 27.9 that have taken effect; any amount they do not need stays in the Reserve. No transfer shall be made in a year the balance is below its target, including during and after a draw, until it is refilled.
- **Above the target:** remaining deposits shall go to a Capital Investment Account, which may fund one-time capital projects, and debt service on borrowing for long-lived assets under Section 27.7, but never recurring programs.
- **Investment:** assets shall be held in broad, passive index funds managed by private managers selected through competitive bidding, with no voting rights in any company.
- **Drawing:** the stabilization balance may be drawn only when the recession trigger in Section 1.2 is met. Draws shall first pay for automatic extended unemployment benefits (Section 4.4.4); any remainder may fund only temporary stabilization measures enacted for that downturn.
- **Refilling:** once recovery is confirmed under Section 1.2, deposits shall restore the balance to its target before anything goes to the Capital Investment Account.
- **No other claims:** apart from the uses listed under Drawing, no program, fund, or endowment may draw on the Reserve.

### 27.6 Funded Spending Cap \[Statute\]

The baseline spending cap in Section 1.2 may rise only by the independently scored revenue that a newly enacted program brings with it under Section 27.3.

### 27.7 Borrowing Rule \[Statute\]

Borrowing may finance long-lived productive assets, such as infrastructure, grid and transmission, public housing, and research facilities. Borrowing shall not finance recurring obligations. Debt service on such borrowing is paid through the Capital Investment Account (Section 27.5), is not a General Fund phase-in item, and limits new borrowing to what the account can service.

### 27.8 No Double Counting \[Statute\]

Each dollar of revenue or savings shall be counted once. Cost estimates shall show net figures. For example, Universal Health Insurance estimates shall count only the drug-negotiation savings that remain after the Innovation Fund's share.

### 27.9 Phased Implementation \[Statute\]

Programs shall be phased in by feasibility: first low-cost statutory reforms (such as voter registration, cannabis legalization, and anti-corruption measures), then revenue measures, and finally the largest new programs (health insurance, childcare, tuition-free higher education, K-12 equity funding, student loan reform, and expanded farm risk protection). No program in the final group shall take effect until the revenue assigned to it has been enacted and independently scored.

**General Fund priority order.** Recurring programs paid from the General Fund shall be phased in in this order, and no program in a later tier shall take effect before every program in the earlier tiers is fully funded:

1. **Income security (Chapter 4)**, in this order:
   - SSI asset and income rules, and the SNAP changes;
   - the Earned Income Tax Credit for workers without children;
   - full refundability of the Child Tax Credit (Section 4.2.1);
   - the young child supplement to the Child Tax Credit (Section 4.2.2);
   - training grants under Universal Adjustment Assistance and any triggered apprenticeship expansion, within one capped amount (Sections 3.6.7 to 3.6.9);
   - alongside these, the expanded Disabled Access Credit for small businesses (Section 13.5.1), paired with the digital accessibility deadlines.
2. **Housing and early childhood**, in this order:
   - housing vouchers for people experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness (Section 8.3.3);
   - emergency rental assistance and the right to counsel in eviction cases (Section 8.3.6);
   - universal preschool (Section 7.1);
   - the childcare cost caps (Section 3.4);
   - the eldercare cost caps (Section 3.4).
3. **Higher education and K-12**, in this order:
   - tuition-free public higher education (Section 7.3);
   - existing student-loan reform (Section 7.3.1);
   - K-12 equity funding (Section 7.2.1).
4. **Universal housing vouchers** for extremely low-income renters (Section 8.3.3), phased in by stages.

Programs in tiers 3 and 4 may also be paid from the smoothed transfer in Section 27.5. That transfer shall never pay for tiers 1 and 2, so a fall in wealth-tax revenue, or a court ruling against the tax, can delay only the later tiers.

**Fixed-budget programs.** Research expansion, national service, the public housing developer's operations, state rent stabilization grants, and the enhancements to the National Farm Risk Protection Program are not ranked in the tiers. They follow these rules:

- each continues at its current-law funding level;
- expansion above current-law levels occurs only after the first tier is fully funded;
- expansion remains subject to a fixed statutory ceiling for each program and to the spending cap in Section 27.6;
- construction of long-lived assets uses the Capital Investment Account (Section 27.5) and the borrowing rule in Section 27.7, and its debt service is paid through that account rather than waiting in the tier order;
- the National Farm Risk Protection Program preserves current-law crop insurance commitments, and only its enhanced benefits are subject to the ceiling.

Paid leave is financed by its own trust fund (Section 3.3) and is not a General Fund program. Appendix B sets out the legislative path for each program. Programs with their own funding (Universal Health Insurance, Social Security, unemployment insurance) are phased in on their own schedules. Revenue from the loss of tax benefits under the rent stabilization floor (Section 8.2.3) goes to the General Fund and is not assigned to any program.

### 27.10 Independent Costing \[Statute\]

The Congressional Budget Office and the Joint Committee on Taxation shall estimate the cost of each major program and the yield of each revenue measure, and the estimates shall be published with the implementing legislation. The Government Accountability Office shall review each major program at least once every ten years (Section 14.2.1).

### 27.11 Combined Tax Rate Disclosure \[Statute\]

Each edition of this platform, and each revenue bill implementing it, shall publish the combined top marginal rate on wages, pass-through income, and capital gains, counting income tax, Social Security and Medicare contributions, Universal Health Insurance contributions, and the Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax. The current disclosure is the table in Section 2.12. The combined federal rate on wages and self-employment income shall not exceed 60 percent (Section 2.8).

### 27.12 Honest Limit

Dollar estimates are not yet included in this edition; the costing table is maintained in `data/costing.csv` and will be filled from published independent estimates. Wealth-tax and carbon revenues are deliberately kept away from recurring programs, so the recurring programs depend on the federal tax fairness measures. Those measures are set out in Chapter 2 (Sections 2.6 to 2.10) as proposed rates. If independent scoring shows less revenue than the recurring programs need, the programs phase in more slowly; the rates do not rise automatically (Section 2.11).
