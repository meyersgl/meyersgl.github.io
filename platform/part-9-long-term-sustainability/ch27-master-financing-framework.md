## Chapter 27: Master Financing Framework

*This chapter is the single authority for every revenue and spending rule in the platform. Decision records: 0004–0008.*

### 27.1 Guiding Principle

A platform is only as credible as its funding. Every major commitment in this document shall have an identified source of revenue, an independent cost estimate, and a realistic implementation sequence.

Two rules follow. Each source of revenue shall be assigned to exactly one use, so that no dollar is promised twice. Recurring obligations shall be paid only from stable revenue; revenue that swings with markets or shrinks over time shall pay only for reserves, one-time investments, or time-limited programs.

### 27.2 Funding Source Requirement \[Statute\]

Before any major new program in this platform takes effect, an independent cost estimate and its assigned revenue stream under Section 27.3 shall be published. Programs shall be sequenced so that new obligations do not outpace identified revenue.

### 27.5 Sovereign Wealth Reserve \[Statute\]

The Sovereign Wealth Reserve (Section 1.3) shall exist only to stabilize the economy in recessions.

- **Funding:** all Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue shall be deposited in the Reserve until its stabilization balance reaches a target fixed in statute as a share of GDP.
- **Above the target:** further deposits shall go to a Capital Investment Account, which may fund one-time capital projects only and never recurring programs.
- **Investment:** assets shall be held in broad, passive index funds managed by private managers selected through competitive bidding, with no voting rights in any company.
- **Drawing:** the stabilization balance may be drawn only when the recession trigger in Section 1.2 is met.
- **Refilling:** once recovery is confirmed under Section 1.2, deposits shall restore the balance to its target before anything goes to the Capital Investment Account.
- **No other claims:** no program, fund, or endowment may draw on the Reserve.

### 27.6 Funded Spending Cap \[Statute\]

The baseline spending cap in Section 1.2 may rise only by the independently scored revenue that a newly enacted program brings with it under Section 27.3.

### 27.7 Borrowing Rule \[Statute\]

Borrowing may finance long-lived productive assets, such as infrastructure, grid and transmission, and research facilities. Borrowing shall not finance recurring obligations.

### 27.8 No Double Counting \[Statute\]

Each dollar of revenue or savings shall be counted once. Cost estimates shall show net figures. For example, Universal Health Insurance estimates shall count only the drug-negotiation savings that remain after the Innovation Fund's share.

### 27.9 Phased Implementation \[Statute\]

Programs shall be phased in by feasibility: first low-cost statutory reforms (such as voter registration, cannabis legalization, and anti-corruption measures), then revenue measures, and finally the largest new programs (health insurance, childcare, tuition-free higher education, K-12 equity funding, student loan reform, and expanded farm risk protection). No program in the final group shall take effect until the revenue assigned to it has been enacted and independently scored.

### 27.10 Independent Costing \[Statute\]

The Congressional Budget Office and the Joint Committee on Taxation shall estimate the cost of each major program and the yield of each revenue measure, and the estimates shall be published with the implementing legislation. The Government Accountability Office shall review each major program at least once every ten years (Section 14.2.1).

### 27.11 Combined Tax Rate Disclosure \[Statute\]

Each edition of this platform, and each revenue bill implementing it, shall publish the combined top marginal rate on wages, pass-through income, and capital gains, counting income tax, Social Security and Medicare contributions, Universal Health Insurance contributions, and the Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax. The current disclosure is the table in Section 2.12. The combined federal rate on wages and self-employment income shall not exceed 60 percent (Section 2.8).

### 27.12 Honest Limit

Dollar estimates are not yet included in this edition; the costing table is maintained in `data/costing.csv` and will be filled from published independent estimates. Wealth-tax and carbon revenues are deliberately kept away from recurring programs, so the recurring programs depend on the federal tax fairness measures. Those measures are set out in Chapter 2 (Sections 2.6 to 2.10) as proposed rates. If independent scoring shows less revenue than the recurring programs need, the programs phase in more slowly; the rates do not rise automatically (Section 2.11).
