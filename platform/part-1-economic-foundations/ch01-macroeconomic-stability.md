## Chapter 1: Macroeconomic Stability

### 1.1 Steady Economic Growth \[Statute\]

The Federal Reserve shall pursue maximum employment and stable prices by targeting a path for the level of nominal GDP that rises 4 percent a year. When nominal GDP falls below the path, as in a recession, monetary policy shall aim to return it to the path, not merely to resume 4 percent growth from the lower level, so that lost ground is made up. With real growth of about 2 percent a year, the path implies inflation of about 2 percent. The Federal Reserve shall set the path's starting level, publish the gap between nominal GDP and the path with each policy decision, and explain in writing to Congress any gap that persists for more than a year. The Federal Reserve shall choose the tools used to reach the path.

### 1.2 Recession Emergency Valve \[Statute\]

Baseline federal spending shall be capped at its share of GDP in the fiscal year the cap is enacted, as measured by the Congressional Budget Office. The cap shall be suspended automatically, within the first quarter of a downturn, when a rules-based recession indicator (such as an early-warning unemployment trigger of the Sahm Rule type) is met and core inflation, measured by the personal consumption expenditures price index excluding food and energy, is no more than 0.5 percentage points above the Federal Reserve's longer-run inflation goal. The inflation threshold prevents fiscal stimulus from compounding a supply-shock or stagflationary crisis. The cap shall return only after a sustained, multi-quarter confirmation of economic recovery, so that spending is not withdrawn prematurely. Outside a downturn, the cap may rise only by the independently scored revenue that a newly enacted program brings with it (Section 27.6).

### 1.3 Sovereign Wealth Reserve \[Statute\]

A protected sovereign reserve fund shall exist solely to stabilize the economy during recessions. It shall be funded by Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue (Section 2.1) up to a stabilization target of 3 percent of GDP, about \$1 trillion at today's GDP, drawn only when the recession trigger in Section 1.2 is met, and refilled after recovery. Once the target is reached, a smoothed transfer goes to the General Fund for programs in tiers 3 and 4 only, and the rest goes to a Capital Investment Account for one-time capital projects. No other program, fund, or endowment may claim the Reserve, except the automatic extended unemployment benefits in Section 4.4.4. Full rules are in Section 27.5.

### 1.4 Honest Limits

- **Nominal GDP level targeting is untested.** No major central bank has formally adopted it. It works only if markets believe the Federal Reserve will make up shortfalls, and after a supply shock, such as an oil price spike, returning to the path can mean tolerating higher inflation for a time.
- **Congress may set the Federal Reserve's goal, but doing so is contested.** In *Trump v. Cook* (2026) the Supreme Court preserved removal protection for Federal Reserve governors, on narrow grounds \[1\]. Setting the target in statute is within Congress's power; critics will argue that it invites future Congresses to change the target for political reasons.
- **A statutory cap binds only until Congress changes it.** Spending caps set in statute have been raised by later budget agreements, and nothing in this chapter prevents a future Congress from doing the same.
- **The cap's scope must be defined.** Health and retirement spending tend to grow faster than GDP as the population ages. Implementing legislation must define which spending counts as baseline; if programs paid from their own trust funds are included, holding total spending to a fixed share of GDP would squeeze everything else.
- **Recession triggers can misfire.** Rules of the Sahm type have signaled downturns that did not follow. The inflation condition and the multi-quarter recovery test limit, but do not remove, the risk of lifting the cap too early or too late.
- **The Reserve depends on the wealth tax.** It fills only from Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue. If that tax raises less than expected or is struck down, the Reserve may take many years to reach 3 percent of GDP or never reach it, and the extended unemployment benefits in Section 4.4.4, the smoothed transfer, and the Capital Investment Account all wait (Appendix E).
- **A large public fund invites raids.** A reserve near \$1 trillion will draw proposals to spend it on other purposes. Section 27.5 bars other claims, but a later Congress can amend it.
