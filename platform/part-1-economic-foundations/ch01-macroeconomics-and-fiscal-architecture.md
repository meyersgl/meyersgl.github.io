## CHAPTER 1: MACROECONOMICS & FISCAL ARCHITECTURE

### 1.1 Steady Economic Growth \[Statute\]

The central bank shall maintain a stable nominal GDP growth target of approximately 4-5 percent annually to reduce recession severity and maintain employment stability.

### 1.2 Recession Emergency Valve \[Statute\]

Baseline federal spending shall be capped relative to the size of the economy. The cap shall be suspended automatically, within the first quarter of a downturn, when a rules-based recession indicator (such as an early-warning unemployment trigger of the Sahm Rule type) is met and core inflation is at or below a defined target threshold. The inflation threshold prevents fiscal stimulus from compounding a supply-shock or stagflationary crisis. The cap shall return only after a sustained, multi-quarter confirmation of economic recovery, so that spending is not withdrawn prematurely. Outside a downturn, the cap may rise only by the independently scored revenue that a newly enacted program brings with it (Section 22.6).

### 1.3 Sovereign Wealth Reserve \[Statute\]

A protected sovereign reserve fund shall exist solely to stabilize the economy during recessions. It shall be funded by Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax revenue (Section 1.4) up to a stabilization target fixed in statute, drawn only when the recession trigger in Section 1.2 is met, and refilled after recovery. Revenue above the target shall go to a Capital Investment Account for one-time capital projects only. No other program, fund, or endowment may claim the Reserve. Full rules are in Section 22.5.

### 1.4 Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax \[Statute – High Resistance\]

A tax shall apply to wealth above an inflation-indexed threshold of \$50 million, in marginal brackets rather than a single cliff, excluding primary residences, retirement accounts, and ordinary small businesses. The tax shall be designed as closely as possible to a tax on gains, to reduce constitutional risk under the Direct Tax Clause.

- Publicly traded assets: unrealized gains shall be taxed annually using daily market closing prices.

- Illiquid assets (private companies and closely held businesses): taxed at realization.

- Loans and synthetic liquidity: any large loan collateralized by covered assets, and any transaction that provides liquidity referencing an asset's value without a taxable sale (including prepaid variable forward contracts, equity collars, and swaps), shall count as a realization event. This broadens the constructive-sale precedent in Internal Revenue Code Section 1259.

Implementation note: because this rule is written broadly on purpose, expect multi-year litigation over the boundary between legitimate hedging and taxable liquidity extraction.

#### 1.4.1 Enhanced Anti-Evasion Framework \[Statute\]

Mandatory beneficial ownership reporting, asset aggregation requirements, global disclosure standards, concealment penalties, and strengthened exit-tax provisions shall prevent avoidance schemes. Aggregation rules shall cover trusts, family entities, and minor children.

### 1.5 Liquidity Smoothing Mechanism \[Statute\]

Covered taxpayers may satisfy certain obligations through transfers of non-voting equity. Equity received shall be held in a separate passive account, shall carry no government voting or control rights, shall not count toward the Sovereign Wealth Reserve's recession-stabilization balance, and shall be sold down on a defined schedule.

### 1.6 Productive Capital Exemption \[Statute\]

Borrowing against business equity is exempt from the realization rule in Section 1.4 only when the proceeds are contributed directly to the operating business and used for productive capital expenditure (such as payroll growth, infrastructure, manufacturing expansion, or research and development), verified through audited tracing of funds. Loan proceeds used for personal consumption, owner distributions, or the purchase of unrelated assets are realization events. The exemption shall be capped as a share of the borrower's covered wealth to prevent abuse.

### 1.7 Targeted IRS Audits \[Statute\]

A dedicated High-Net-Worth and Complex-Entity Enforcement Division shall be statutorily ring-fenced. Its funding shall be restricted by appropriations rider to audits of filers above the Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax threshold and of large or complex corporate and partnership structures and shall not be used to audit ordinary filers. The IRS shall publicly report audit rates by income and wealth bracket, and the division shall be reauthorized every five years based on performance data.

### 1.8 Income Tax Overhaul \[Statute\]

Federal incentives shall encourage states to move toward more progressive taxation and away from highly regressive sales tax dependence.
