## Chapter 16: National Infrastructure, Industrial Capacity & Economic Resilience

### 16.1 Guiding Principle

Infrastructure and productive capacity are the foundations of national prosperity.

Markets create growth, but growth depends upon reliable transportation networks, energy systems, communications infrastructure, supply chains, and productive industrial capacity.

A modern economy requires both.

### 16.2 National Infrastructure & Strategic Capacity Act \[Statute\]

Federal policy shall support long-term investment in infrastructure and productive capacity necessary for future economic growth.

#### 16.2.1 National Infrastructure Renewal Program

Investment priorities include:

- Roads

- Bridges

- Ports

- Airports

- Freight rail

- Inland waterways

- Public transit

- Water systems

- Wastewater infrastructure

Infrastructure spending should emphasize long-term maintenance and modernization.

#### 16.2.2 State Partnership Framework

Federal programs should operate through cooperative state and local partnerships whenever practical.

Funding decisions shall be guided by:

- Need

- Economic impact

- Safety

- Resilience

rather than political considerations.

### 16.3 Grid Modernization & Energy Reliability \[Statute\]

The electrical grid shall be modernized to improve:

- Reliability

- Capacity

- Cybersecurity

- Interregional connectivity

- Disaster resilience

Priority shall be given to projects improving national energy stability.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 15 Climate & Energy.

### 16.4 Strategic Industrial Capacity Initiative \[Statute – High Resistance\]

The United States shall maintain domestic capabilities in strategically important industries.

Priority sectors include:

- Semiconductors

- Biotechnology

- Pharmaceuticals

- Aerospace

- Robotics

- Advanced manufacturing

- Nuclear technologies

The objective is resilience rather than economic isolation.

#### 16.4.1 Competitive Neutrality Principle

Government support shall remain open to firms of varying size whenever possible.

Industrial policy should strengthen competition rather than entrench existing monopolies.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 4 Market Competition & Economic Opportunity.

### 16.5 Supply Chain Resilience Program \[Statute\]

Federal agencies shall identify vulnerabilities within supply chains affecting:

- National security

- Public health

- Food security

- Energy reliability

- Manufacturing

The government may support:

- Strategic reserves

- Diversified sourcing

- Domestic production capacity

when excessive dependence creates national risk.

This section is the central supply-chain program. Sections 2.4, 5.2.4, and 17.6 apply it to domestic sourcing, trade treatment, and emergency reserves.

### 16.6 Workforce Development Partnership \[Statute\]

Infrastructure and industrial investment shall be coordinated with workforce development.

Programs may include:

- Apprenticeships

- Community-college partnerships

- Trade certification programs

- Employer-sponsored training

Cross-reference: See Chapter 2 Labor, Family & Economic Justice.

### 16.7 Domestic Production Preference \[Statute\]

Federal projects receiving major public support should prioritize domestic production whenever:

- Quality remains competitive

- Price differences remain reasonable

- Supply needs can be met

The goal is strengthening domestic productive capacity while maintaining competition.

### 16.8 National Permitting Reform \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Major infrastructure projects shall receive coordinated review procedures supported by funded permitting staff.

**One Project, One Review**

Federal agencies shall coordinate reviews to reduce duplication while preserving:

- Environmental protections

- Public safety standards

- Property-right protections

Efficient permitting is not the same as weaker standards.
