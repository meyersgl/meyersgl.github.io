## Chapter 9: Security, Immigration & Justice

### 9.1 Guiding Principle

A secure nation requires strong institutions, lawful immigration, effective law enforcement, and respect for constitutional rights.

National security and individual liberty are complementary, not contradictory.

### 9.2 Defensive Foreign Policy \[Statute / Executive\]

The United States shall maintain military capabilities sufficient to:

- Defend the nation

- Protect allies

- Secure global trade

- Deter aggression

The nation shall end foreign regime-change operations and shall reduce reliance on:

- Long-term occupations

- Open-ended military engagements

whenever practical.

**Offshore Balancing Posture**

The United States shall shift from permanent foreign land bases toward maritime and over-the-horizon capabilities that protect trade routes and international waters, and shall ask regional allies to share local defense burdens. Reduced forward presence and the end of regime-change operations are severable commitments; either may proceed without the other.

**Peace Through Strength**

Military strength exists primarily to prevent war, not seek it.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 18 Defense Procurement & Military Modernization.

### 9.3 Immigration Reorganization Framework \[Statute\]

Federal immigration enforcement shall be reorganized to separate criminal enforcement from civil immigration administration.

The Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency shall be dissolved in its current form, and its functions shall be reassigned as described below.

**Criminal Enforcement Division**

Specialized units shall focus on:

- Human trafficking

- Cartels

- Organized smuggling

- Transnational criminal activity

**Civil Immigration Administration**

Administrative functions shall transition to a professional civilian agency emphasizing:

- Efficiency

- Due process

- Customer service

- Legal compliance

The objective is improved effectiveness without weakening enforcement.

### 9.4 Citizenship Pathways \[Statute\]

Priority shall be given to lawful pathways for:

- Dreamers

- Agricultural workers

- Long-term productive residents

upon satisfaction of established legal requirements.

See Section 11.2.5 for the full pathway provisions.

### 9.5 Ban on Private Prisons \[Statute\]

The federal government shall terminate contracts with for-profit prison and immigration-detention operators.

The deprivation of liberty should never become a profit-generating business model.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 10 Criminal Justice Modernization.

### 9.6 Federal Cannabis Legalization \[Statute\]

Cannabis shall be removed from the federal controlled-substances schedule.

States shall retain authority over regulation within their jurisdictions.

Federal authorities shall expunge qualifying non-violent federal cannabis records.

### 9.7 Civil Addiction Courts & Public Health Stabilization \[Statute\]

Low-level possession offenses shall be routed through civil diversion systems rather than traditional criminal incarceration.

Treatment participation must remain voluntary.

Completion of civil diversion shall result in expungement of the related record.

**Parity Lock**

Diversion systems require adequate treatment capacity before activation.

**Proximity Lock**

Treatment access must remain geographically accessible to affected populations.

Addiction Hotspots shall receive priority for treatment capacity so that diversion can activate there first.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 3 Human Infrastructure.
