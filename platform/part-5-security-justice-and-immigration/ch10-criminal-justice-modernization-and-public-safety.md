## Chapter 10: Criminal Justice Modernization & Public Safety

### 10.1 Guiding Principle

The first responsibility of the criminal justice system is public safety.

The second responsibility is rehabilitation whenever rehabilitation is realistically possible.

Punishment alone is not a public-safety strategy.

Reducing future crime is the ultimate objective.

### 10.2 Violent Crime Priority Standard \[Statute\]

Federal law enforcement shall prioritize:

- Violent offenders

- Sexual offenders

- Human traffickers

- Organized criminal enterprises

- Repeat predatory offenders

Resources should focus on individuals who pose the greatest danger to the public.

### 10.3 Rehabilitation First Framework \[Statute\]

Whenever public safety allows, non-violent offenders should have access to:

- Education

- Workforce development

- Mental-health treatment

- Substance-abuse treatment

- Reentry support

The purpose of rehabilitation is reducing future victimization.

### 10.4 Sentencing Review Commission \[Statute\]

An independent commission shall periodically review sentencing policies for:

- Fairness

- Consistency

- Cost effectiveness

- Public-safety outcomes

- Recidivism reduction

Recommendations shall be reported publicly.

### 10.5 Prison Reentry Initiative \[Statute\]

Individuals successfully completing sentences shall receive access to:

- Employment assistance

- Housing support

- Credential recognition

- Educational opportunities

- Workforce development

The objective is successful reintegration into society.

### 10.6 Permanent Ban on For-Profit Prisons \[Statute\]

See Section 9.5. No federal correctional or immigration-detention facility shall be operated for private profit.

Human incarceration should never create a business incentive to increase imprisonment.

### 10.7 Public Safety Outcome Standard \[Statute\]

Justice policies shall be evaluated based upon:

- Crime reduction

- Victim protection

- Public safety

- Reduced recidivism

rather than political ideology.

Cross-reference: See Chapter 7 Evidence-Based Governance.

### 10.8 Victim Rights Framework \[Statute\]

Victims of violent crime shall receive enhanced access to:

- Counseling services

- Legal support

- Restitution assistance

- Victim advocacy services

The justice system must protect both communities and victims while preserving constitutional rights for the accused.

### 10.9 Ending Qualified Immunity \[Statute\]

Qualified immunity, a judicially created doctrine, shall be eliminated by statute clarifying 42 U.S.C. Section 1983. A hybrid accountability model shall apply: individual officer civil liability shall be capped (for example, at \$25,000), and the employing municipality shall cover remaining damages unless the officer acted in bad faith. This holds departments accountable without exposing officers to personal bankruptcy over split-second judgment calls.

### 10.10 Firearm Safety \[Statute – High Resistance\]

Background checks shall be required for all firearm sales and transfers, including private and online sales, with narrow exceptions (such as transfers between close family members).

Extreme risk protection (red flag) orders shall allow a court to temporarily remove firearms from a person who poses a danger to themselves or others. Federal law and federal funding incentives shall support these orders, with due-process protections: notice, a hearing, a clear evidentiary standard, time limits, and a defined path to restore firearm rights.

Honest limit: these measures do not ban any category of firearm. They will face Second Amendment challenges, so due-process protections are essential, and enforcement depends on background-check system funding and compliance.
