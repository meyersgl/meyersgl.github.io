# Changelog

Newest first. Each entry lists what changed and which decision records it follows.

## Unreleased: v1.2 Complete Edition (in progress)

### Chapter 2: Federal Tax Fairness (decision 0022)
- New Sections 2.6 to 2.13:
  - restored 39.6% top rate
  - capital gains and dividends taxed at ordinary rates above $1M
  - carried interest taxed as ordinary income
  - gains taxed at death above a $5M per person exclusion, with deferral for family farms and businesses
  - a rule that no gain is taxed twice
  - a 60% ceiling on the combined federal rate on wages
  - corporate minimum tax raised to 21%, with research and production credits protected
  - estate tax exemption of $5M (indexed) at 40%, with avoidance tools closed
  - proposed rates published subject to scoring
  - the combined-rate table required by Section 27.11
  - an honest-limits section
- Section 5.1.2 and Sections 27.11 and 27.12 updated to point to Chapter 2 and the wage ceiling.

### Chapter 4: Income Security & the Safety Net (decision 0023)
- New Sections 4.1 to 4.9:
  - a 50% combined phase-out limit, one application, and an annual cliff report
  - a fully refundable Child Tax Credit with a $1,000 supplement for children under 6
  - an expanded EITC for workers without children
  - federal minimum standards for unemployment insurance, with automatic extended benefits in recessions
  - SNAP work-expectation exemptions for people with disabilities and caregivers, automatic verification, and a 90-day grace period
  - SSI asset and income reforms, and a Disability Insurance earnings offset
  - repeal of the Medicaid work requirement and a voluntary UHI Partnership Compact
  - a funding table and honest limits
- Section 27.9 now sets the General Fund priority order (income security, then preschool and childcare, then tuition-free college). Sections 27.3 to 27.5, 1.3 and 5.1.6 updated to match.
- Executive Summary: new Income Security section and the phase-in order.

### Chapter 13: Civil Rights & Equal Protection (decision 0024)
- New Sections 13.2 to 13.9:
  - Voting Rights Act restoration: a private right to sue, a vote-denial test, an evidentiary standard for intent in dilution cases, race-neutral remedies, known-practices preclearance, and timely-relief rules
  - protected traits listed separately, with statutory definitions, across six areas of law
  - disparate impact codified
  - religious liberty limits, including that for-profit corporations do not exercise religion (reversing *Hobby Lobby*)
  - digital and AI accessibility, *Olmstead* written into statute, accommodation reforms, and an end to subminimum wages
  - pay data, salary ranges, and equal-pay proof rules
  - enforcement designed to work under *Slaughter*, with no forced arbitration
  - a funding table and honest limits
- Cross-references:
  - Section 12.4: multi-member districts as a Voting Rights Act remedy
  - Appendix A: an affirmative right to vote
  - Appendix C: three new rows
  - Sections 27.4 and 27.9: Disabled Access Credit in tier 1
  - Section 5.1.7: the long-term care benefit must include home- and community-based services
- Executive Summary: new Civil Rights section.

### Chapter 16: Officer Accountability (decision 0029)
- Section 16.9 rewritten as one accountability system:
  - qualified immunity ended for state and local officers (16.9.1);
  - a statutory cause of action against anyone acting under color of federal law (16.9.2);
  - Federal Tort Claims Act repairs (16.9.3);
  - authorized state remedies limited to the federal constitutional standard (16.9.4);
  - a $25,000 indexed personal share with no indirect reimbursement (16.9.5);
  - a two-year transition (16.9.6);
  - public settlement reporting (16.9.7).
- New Sections:
  - 16.13 Civil Rights Risk Reduction Grants;
  - 16.14 pattern-or-practice enforcement;
  - 16.15 funding;
  - 16.16 honest limits;
  - a Sources list.
- Section 27.4, Appendix C, and the Executive Summary updated.

### Chapter 4: Reversing the 2025 SNAP cuts (decision 0028)
- Section 4.5.1 exemptions:
  - restores the exemptions for people experiencing homelessness, veterans, and former foster youth;
  - broadens the caregiver exemption to any child under 18.
- Section 4.5.2 accepts homeless-services records, provider statements, or self-statement.
- Section 4.5.4 restores the 50% federal share of administrative costs.
- New Section 4.5.5 restores:
  - Thrifty Food Plan re-evaluations;
  - utility and internet deductions;
  - SNAP-Ed.
- New Section 4.5.6 restores eligibility for refugees, asylees, and other pre-2025 qualified immigrants.
- Honest limits and Executive Summary updated.

### Chapter 8: Housing & Homelessness (decision 0027)
- Rent caps moved to a new Section 8.2, Anti-Gouging Rent Stabilization:
  - inflation plus 5%, never more than 10%, for landlords with 50 or more units
  - enforced through depreciation, like-kind exchange, and federally backed loan conditions
  - vacancy reset, just-cause eviction, fair-return petitions
  - tenant lawsuits, state attorney general enforcement, and IRS rent-roll matching
  - grants for state and local caps
- New Section 8.3, Ending Homelessness:
  - Housing First in statute with a 70% permanent housing floor
  - recovery housing as a choice
  - voucher guarantee for people experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness
  - "adequate offer" conditions on encampment enforcement
  - a due-process floor for civil commitment
  - emergency rental assistance, right to counsel, and eviction-record sealing
- New Sections 8.4 (funding) and 8.5 (honest limits). Former Sections 8.1.6 and 8.1.7 are now 8.1.5 and 8.1.6.
- Sections 27.4 and 27.9 updated: housing vouchers and eviction prevention lead tier 2, and a new tier 4 holds universal vouchers. Appendix C and the Executive Summary updated.
- Decision index (decisions/README.md) brought up to date through 0027.
- After review: added a supportive-services standard to Section 8.3.1, tied the adequate-offer accessibility rule to the ADA and Section 504, deferred tier ordering in Section 8.4 to Section 27.9, and sourced the chronic homelessness figure to CRS.

### Chapter 6: Public Coverage and Conscience (decision 0026)
- New Section 6.2, Public Coverage of Abortion Care:
  - Universal Health Insurance covers abortion care for any reason through 24 weeks and 6 days
  - after that, coverage for life or health, fatal fetal conditions, and rape or incest
  - rape and incest covered at any lawful point, with no reporting requirement
  - a request-date rule, early diagnosis at no cost, and written notice of the 24-week date
  - Hyde and related domestic riders ended, with benefits under a permanent appropriation
  - interim Medicaid coverage at 100% federal cost
- New Section 6.3, Conscience, Patient Information, and Emergency Care:
  - individual conscience protection, with duties attached
  - an enforceable right to know one's options, with a written referral to at least two providers and a required statement
  - institutional limits that match Chapter 13
  - EMTALA stabilizing treatment written into statute, explicitly including abortion care
  - protection for clinicians: no legal review before emergency care, preemption of state penalties, removal to federal court, and attorney fees
  - pharmacy duties
  - protection in both directions, with private enforcement
- New Section 6.4, Honest Limits (replaces 6.1.5)
- Section 5.1 and the UHI white paper's benefit line now point to the coverage terms in Section 6.2

### Chapter 3: Technology and AI at Work (decision 0025)
- New Section 3.6 (3.6.1 to 3.6.12):
  - a human decision-maker for firing, discipline, and deactivation
  - notice, explanation, and data access
  - annual bias audits by vendors, with shared responsibility
  - surveillance limits, with an accessibility exception
  - 90 days' notice of technological change, and bargaining over it
  - Universal Adjustment Assistance: wage insurance paid from unemployment trust funds, training, and 90-day layoff notice
  - BLS indicators of AI effects on jobs, with an apprenticeship trigger
  - a shared, capped funding amount in tier 1, step 4
  - no tax on automation
  - enforcement, a minimum standard that does not preempt stronger state laws, and honest limits
- Cross-references updated: Sections 3.5.2, 4.4.2, 4.8, 13.5.2, 19.6, 27.4, and 27.9
- Executive Summary: new Technology and Work section

## v1.1 Consistency Edition (November 12, 2026)

Those styles are now added and both PDFs reissued under the same filenames. The text did not change. The Full Platform drops from 132 to 104 pages because the tables are now laid out properly.

### Release
- Version 1.1 PDFs of the Full Platform, Executive Summary, One Pager, and the three white papers added to docs/ (the v1 files stay in place).
- Executive Summary and One Pager now kept as markdown in summaries/. The summary renames the Millionaire Fair-Share Tax to the Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax, replaces the revenue list with the one-use-per-stream rule from Chapter 27, and aligns the Social Security, health, and AI descriptions with the white papers.
- Build now renders PDFs from the Word files with LibreOffice so they keep the Word styling, with the table of contents refreshed (build/docx2pdf.py).
- Title page, Document Information, Version History, white-paper front matter, CITATION.cff, and the website edition notes updated to v1.1.

### Design rules and settled decisions (P2c)
- New shared definitions: Dominance Threshold, Strategic Protection Test, Coordinated Review Standard, Capital Investment vs. Recurring Obligation, and Hosted vs. Open-Weight Models. The Living Wage definition is now the benchmark for the wage percentage in 3.1.
- Competition rules are conduct-based. Extra platform duties (9.2.2, 9.2.3, 9.2.5), housing investor limits (8.1.3), and farmland review (10.1.5) apply only above the Dominance Threshold.
- Tariffs (11.2.2) require authority expressly granted by Congress and must meet the Strategic Protection Test, citing *Learning Resources v. Trump*. Trade protection, the critical-supplies list, domestic-production preferences, and defense sourcing must also meet the test.
- Independent bodies redesigned after *Trump v. Slaughter*: GAO conducts ten-year reviews; Inspector General removal requires 30 days' notice and acting IGs come from career deputies; a congressional ethics office plus enforcement in the courts replaces the executive-branch commission; career prosecutors are protected; the U.S. Sentencing Commission conducts sentencing review; Innovation Fund awards go through peer review; the critical-supplies list is recommended by a national-laboratory office and issued by rule (decision 0021).
- 23.2: offshore balancing narrowed to no new permanent bases without congressional authorization, plus burden-sharing and force-posture reports. Ending regime change stays core (decision 0002).
- 24.12: defense held roughly flat after inflation; procurement savings are reinvested in defense and not counted as platform financing (decision 0012).
- 14.2.5 one-in-one-out is limited to paperwork; 14.5 pilots are required only for untested designs.
- Legal tags: 12.5 is flagged Constitutionally Contested with an honest-limit note; 12.7 is tagged Statute / Constitutional Amendment; the new flag is added to Legal Classifications; Appendix A's list of future amendments is reconciled with 15.11.
- New Appendix C: Institutional & Regulatory Design Standards.

### Deduplication (P2b)
- The congressional stock-trading ban and insider-trading penalties move from 12.7 into 15.2.4, alongside the other ethics rules. 12.7 becomes Campaign Finance Reform.
- Permitting now has one rule, in 21.9: review is not weakened, and delays are cut through staff, coordination and timelines. Section 20.6 (energy infrastructure) and 8.1.2 (housing) now apply that rule instead of restating it.
- Supply chains: 21.8 and 24.3 point to the central program in 21.6 (and 21.5).
- Space: 18.9.2 covers research partnerships only; space security programs are governed by 24.8.
- National service: because public tuition is already free, the service benefit now covers living costs, apprenticeships, certifications, graduate study, and student-loan repayment. Participants get health coverage through Universal Health Insurance.
- Principles: the Final Clause now refers to all ten Governing Principles rather than a partial list of five. The Statutory Interpretation Principle keeps its ranked order and now says it ranks Governing Principles for resolving statutory ambiguity.
- A one-page Platform Overview replaces the 415-line Executive Summary that was embedded in the platform. The standalone Executive Summary is unchanged.

### Structure (P2a)
- The platform now uses the v1.1 structure of 27 chapters in nine Parts (decision 0003). Front matter comes first: Executive Summary, Legal Classifications, Governing Principles (formerly Appendix A), and Definitions. The v1.0 Appendix B is now Appendix A.
- Chapter 9 is split up: its foreign-policy section moves to Chapter 23, immigration to Chapter 17, and prisons, cannabis and addiction courts to Chapter 16. Its guiding principle becomes the Part V introduction. The duplicate sections on private prisons (old 9.5 and 10.6) and on citizenship pathways (old 9.4 and 11.2.5) are each merged into one.
- Sections moved: tax measures (old 1.4–1.8) to the new Chapter 2, Federal Tax Fairness; welfare-cliff reform to the new Chapter 4; health, reproductive health, education, and housing into their own chapters; privacy and right to repair to Chapter 9; public broadband to Chapter 21; STEM education to Chapter 7; strategic domestic production becomes 21.6.1 under the supply-chain program. Science and Space are combined into Chapter 18.
- Chapter 13, Civil Rights & Equal Protection, is created with its guiding principle. Its statutory provisions are scheduled for v1.2.
- Every section number and cross-reference is updated. New Appendix D maps each v1.0 section to its v1.1 location.
- No policy wording changes, apart from the two merges above.

### White-paper updates (P1)
- All three white papers are now marked as v1.1 working drafts. The v1.0 PDFs in `docs/` are unchanged.
- Universal Health Insurance: wealth-tax revenue is no longer a backstop for health financing (decision 0004).
- Frontier AI Governance: the institutional home (Commerce/NIST) and federal–state roles (a federal floor, plus one national incident-reporting standard) move out of Open Questions and into the proposal, with *Trump v. Slaughter* and Executive Order 14365 cited (decision 0013). The July 2026 incident is updated from the later post-incident reports: about 1,200 agents, and a second affected organization.
- Social Security: the benefit-credit maximum keeps rising with wages (decision 0010). The baseline now explains that most of the deficit increase comes from lower revenue from taxing benefits after the 2025 reconciliation law, plus a lower fertility assumption.

### White-paper alignment (P1)
- Chapter 3, sections 3.2–3.4: Universal Health Insurance matched to its white paper. Changes: a two-layer structure with supplemental insurance; 17 covered categories; no copays or deductibles; a long-term care actuarial study; Trust Fund financing; a statutory Medicare-based fee schedule with a legislative-branch advisory commission; appeals decided by administrative law judges; four implementation phases; an honest-limit note. Drug pricing now includes national negotiation and regulatory exclusivity layered on top of patents. Rural stability now includes grants and minimum service guarantees (decisions 0001, 0004, 0005).
- Chapter 14: Frontier AI matched to its white paper. Changes: a compute trigger plus Algorithmic Capability Index tiers; verified containment; model-weight security; 15-day and 24-hour incident reporting with safe harbor; emergency suspension for hosted systems; independent evaluation and audits; a reasonableness liability standard; oversight at Commerce/NIST with a federal floor and one national reporting standard; an honest-limit note (decisions 0001, 0013). The Frontier AI System definition now matches the paper.
- Chapter 21: Social Security matched to its white paper. Changes: the payroll cap is eliminated; benefits are credited only up to the taxable maximum, which stays indexed to wages; no rate increase below the maximum; pass-through income is covered on the same basis as wages; endowment governance; retirement age kept at 67 with no new means test; no solvency claim until scored. The minimum-benefit increase is removed (decisions 0001, 0009, 0010).
- Website: the health, Social Security, AI, carbon and fiscal summaries now follow the v1.1 working draft. The document-status note and change log say so.

### Financing (P1)
- Chapter 22 rewritten as the Master Financing Framework: every revenue stream assigned to exactly one use, recurring programs funded only from stable revenue, a program funding map, Sovereign Wealth Reserve rules, a funded spending cap, a borrowing rule, no double counting, CBO/JCT costing, and combined tax-rate disclosure (decisions 0004–0008).
- Section 1.2: the spending cap can rise only with scored revenue (decision 0007).
- Section 1.3 and the Definitions: the Sovereign Wealth Reserve is for recession stabilization only and is funded by the wealth tax (decision 0004).
- Section 1.4 and throughout: the Millionaire Fair-Share Tax is renamed the Extreme Wealth Fair-Share Tax (decision 0015).
- Section 3.3: the Innovation Fund is financed by a fixed share of drug-negotiation savings (decision 0005).
- Chapter 15: carbon and methane fee revenue split 80 / 10 / 10; border tariff receipts go to the household dividend; the Transition Fund ends on a set date; the methane fee covers the largest manure lagoons, with livestock and family farms handled through voluntary incentives (decisions 0006, 0011).
- Section 21.4: the Social Security Endowment is funded only from payroll-reform revenue beyond scheduled benefits and reserves, not from the wealth tax (decisions 0001, 0004).
