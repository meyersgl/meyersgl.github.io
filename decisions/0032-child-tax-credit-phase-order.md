## Question
The first affordability test (Appendix E) showed that under conservative assumptions the General Fund revenue left after tier 1, steps 1 and 2 covers full refundability of the Child Tax Credit but not the young child supplement as well. Because a step takes effect only when fully funded, keeping both in one step would hold back full refundability for the poorest children. Should the two be split?

## Decision
Option B. Tier 1 is now:
1. SSI asset and income rules, and the SNAP changes;
2. the EITC for workers without children;
3. full refundability of the Child Tax Credit (Section 4.2.1);
4. the young child supplement (Section 4.2.2);
5. training grants and triggered apprenticeships within one cap, with the expanded Disabled Access Credit.

The policy itself is unchanged: $3,200 per child under 6 once both steps take effect.

## Why
Full refundability reaches the roughly 19 million children who receive less than the full credit today and has the firmest cost estimate. The supplement's estimate is the least certain in tier 1 and should not hold back the larger anti-poverty step.

## Supersedes
The single Child Tax Credit step in decision 0023, and the "tier 1, step 4" placement of training grants in decision 0025 (now step 5).
