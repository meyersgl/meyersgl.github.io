## Question
Chapter 1 left three parameters undefined: the spending cap was "relative to the size of the economy," the recession valve's inflation condition was "a defined target threshold," and the Reserve's stabilization target was "fixed in statute."

## Options considered
1. Fixed numbers now.
2. Leave all three to implementing legislation.
3. Set each by a rule (chosen).

## Decision
- **Spending cap:** baseline federal spending is capped at its share of GDP in the fiscal year the cap is enacted, as measured by the Congressional Budget Office (Section 1.2).
- **Inflation threshold:** the recession valve opens only if core PCE inflation is no more than 0.5 percentage points above the Federal Reserve's longer-run goal (Section 1.2).
- **Reserve target:** 3 percent of GDP, about \$1 trillion at today's GDP (Sections 1.3 and 27.5, Definitions). This matches Appendix E's existing assumption of about \$1 trillion.

## Why
Rules make each test checkable without fixing numbers that would date quickly. The Reserve target makes Appendix E's illustrative assumption the stated rule.

## Trade-off
The cap locks in whatever share of GDP spending happens to have in the enactment year. The inflation threshold depends on a goal the Federal Reserve sets. A 3 percent target may take many years to reach from wealth-tax revenue alone.
