## Question
How should foreign assistance treat abortion and reproductive health care, consistent with Chapter 6?

## Decision
- **F1: A + B.** Repeal the Helms Amendment and the annual appropriations rider by statute. On day one, apply "Helms as written": coverage permitted after rape or incest, when, in reasonable medical judgment, the patient's life or physical or mental health is at risk (matching Chapter 6), and for post-abortion care. Guardrails: services funded only where host-country law permits them; post-abortion care and emergency treatment remain eligible regardless of the legal status of abortion under host-country law.
- **F2: A + B.** A statutory prohibition on future Mexico City or global gag rule policies, including the sentence: "The United States shall not condition eligibility for foreign assistance on an organization's lawful speech, advocacy, counseling, referral practices, or provision of health services conducted with non-United States funds where those activities are lawful under the laws of the host country." Day-one start of notice-and-comment repeal of the three 2026 PHFFA rules. Durability comes from the statute, following the Appendix C approach of writing major rules into statute rather than reversible executive action.
- **F3: approved.** Kemp-Kasten kept, with cutoffs requiring published evidence, written reasons, and notice to Congress. Siljander kept as a neutral rule.
- **F4: approved.** [Statute – High Resistance]; regular order in Appendix B with executive interim steps; existing foreign assistance appropriations, no Chapter 27 cost; restrictions on lawful reproductive health services funded through foreign assistance may be enacted only through amendment of the Foreign Assistance Act and not through annual appropriations riders, enforced by a Senate point of order. Honest limit: a future Congress can waive the point of order.

## Why
Aligns foreign assistance with Chapter 6 while answering the "imposing values" objection through the host-country law rule, and ends the 40-year cycle of executive rescission and reinstatement.
