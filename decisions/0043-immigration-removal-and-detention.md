## Question
Section 17.2 dissolves ICE in its current form but did not say who carries out final orders of removal or what rules govern immigration detention.

## Options considered
1. Move removals to the Justice Department.
2. Leave the question open.
3. Removal by the Civil Immigration Administration, with detention limited by statute (chosen).

## Decision
- **Removal.** The Civil Immigration Administration carries out final orders of removal through civilian officers (new Section 17.2.1).
- **Detention.** Detention is allowed only after an individualized finding, promptly reviewable by an immigration judge, of flight risk or danger that no less restrictive alternative can manage. Alternatives to detention are the default, and laws requiring detention without such a finding are to be amended.
- **Standards.** Minimum detention standards are set in statute and apply equally to government, private, and state or local contract facilities.
- **Enforcement.** Detained people have a private right of action, state attorneys general may sue, and pre-dispute arbitration cannot bar the claims. Judgments against federal officers follow Section 16.9.2, and the Section 27.4 row for officer judgments now cites Section 17.2.1.

## Why
This keeps the separation between civil and criminal enforcement. After *Trump v. Slaughter*, durability comes from statute, a private right of action, state attorney general enforcement, and a ban on pre-dispute arbitration, not from agency independence.

## Trade-off
This is politically the hardest option. Some people released will not appear for hearings, and enforceable standards bring litigation costs.
