## Question
Section 1.1 set a nominal GDP growth target of about 4 to 5 percent a year. Is that the right target and form?

## Options considered
1. Keep the 4 to 5 percent growth-rate target.
2. Keep the dual mandate with nominal GDP gap reporting only.
3. A 4 percent nominal GDP level path (chosen).

## Decision
The Federal Reserve pursues maximum employment and stable prices by targeting a path for the level of nominal GDP that rises 4 percent a year, returning to the path after shortfalls. It sets the starting level, publishes the gap with each policy decision, explains to Congress any gap lasting more than a year, and chooses its own tools (Section 1.1).

## Why
A 4 to 5 percent growth-rate target implies inflation of about 2 to 3 percent if real growth is about 2 percent, and lets recession shortfalls go unrecovered. A level path implies inflation of about 2 percent and makes up lost ground. The target is written as the means of pursuing the existing mandate of maximum employment and stable prices.

## Trade-off
Level targeting is untested at major central banks and harder to explain, and it may require tolerating higher inflation for a time after supply shocks.
