## Question
Dynamic modeling ranks Social Security reforms differently from the Chief Actuary's conventional scores. The Penn Wharton Budget Model's 2026 comparison of seven packages that each restore 75-year solvency found that a benefit-only package raised 2060 GDP by about 12 percent and cut the debt-to-GDP ratio by about 26 percent, while a tax-only package raised GDP by about 0.4 percent and cut the ratio by about 14 percent. Andrew Biggs (American Enterprise Institute) made this the central argument of a September 2026 essay and added two points: payroll tax increases lower cash wages and so reduce income and Medicare tax revenue, and a tax-only fix uses much of the room to raise taxes on high earners. The platform's Social Security plan is tax-only and did not address any of this.

## Decision
- **Keep the plan tax-only.** No benefit cuts, no higher retirement age, no new means test (Section 26.5).
- **State the trade-off openly.** The white paper's new section, Dynamic Effects and the Growth Trade-Off, says the plan's dynamic effect is likely no larger than Penn Wharton's tax-only package and may be smaller, because its taxes fall on wages above the current maximum, pass-through income, and investment income above $1 million.
- **Count what the estimates leave out.** Section 26.6 and Appendix E.4 say the figures are conventional and trust-fund-based, use the Chief Actuary's method rather than CBO's, and omit the General Fund offset from the uncapped employer share, so stable General Fund revenue is somewhat overstated. The offset is not yet estimated.
- **Name the competing claim.** Section 2.13 says Social Security uses much of the top-end tax room, leaving 4.2 points under the wage ceiling (Section 2.8) for the Universal Health Insurance contribution.
- **Ask for a dynamic score.** Added to the white paper's questions and to `REVIEW.md`.

## Why
The growth in benefit-cut packages comes from the cuts. In Penn Wharton's benefit-only package, workers born in 2015 in the lowest-earning fifth would receive about 28 percent less, people near retirement lose far more than under the tax-only package, and the analysis does not estimate effects on old-age poverty. Biggs himself writes that the program's first purpose is protecting Americans against retiring into poverty. The platform treats that purpose as the point of the program and accepts lower long-run growth to keep benefits whole.

## Trade-off
Penn Wharton finds future generations better off over their lifetimes under the benefit-only package; the platform does not dispute this. Critics will say the platform leaves the economy smaller and the debt higher than it could be, and that by spending top-end tax room on Social Security it makes health financing harder. The platform's answer is that this is a choice about values that dynamic analysis informs but does not settle, and that health programs phase in more slowly rather than breach the ceiling.
