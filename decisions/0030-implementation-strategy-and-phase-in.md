## Question
How does the platform pass Congress, and where do the remaining General Fund programs fall in the phase-in order?

## Decision
- **B1 (Senate rules): reform.**
  1. Fully paid-for bills: majority vote after guaranteed debate and amendment votes.
  2. All other legislation: declining cloture threshold (60 votes after introduction, 57 after a fixed delay, 54 after a second delay, then majority after the final delay set by Senate rule).
  3. Minority rights: the minority leader receives a guaranteed number of floor amendments and one guaranteed substitute amendment, each with a recorded vote.
  4. Repeal symmetry: any bill repealing or substantially reducing a law enacted through the paid-for majority procedure is subject to the same procedure.
  5. Qualification: determined by CBO and JCT scoring under Section 27.10.
- **B2 (vehicles): C.** Three tracks in the first year: a reconciliation bill pairing tax fairness revenue with Tier 1, a democracy package that forces the rules question early, and bipartisan bills in parallel. Each plank is assigned a path in Appendix B, Section B.3.
- **B3 (first 100 days): B.** Executive action limited to reversals, implementation, and narrow steps within existing authority.
- **B4 (phase-in): C, hybrid.**
  - R1: fixed-budget programs remain at current-law funding levels.
  - R2: expansion above current-law levels occurs only after Tier 1 is fully funded.
  - R3: expansion remains subject to program ceilings and the overall spending cap.
  - R4: capital construction uses the Capital Investment Account and the borrowing rules in Section 27.7; debt service is paid through the Capital Investment Account and is not a General Fund phase-in item.
  - R5: paid leave becomes a self-financing trust fund supported by a dedicated payroll contribution counted toward the combined-rate ceiling; the rate may adjust within a statutory band to maintain solvency.
  - R6: Farm Risk Protection preserves current-law commitments; enhanced benefits remain subject to fixed statutory ceilings.
  - Tier 2 adds eldercare cost caps. Tier 3 is tuition-free college, then student-loan reform, then K-12 equity funding (college first, as in decision 0023).
- **B5 (classifications):** the [Statute] definition now reflects the 60-vote cloture requirement, and a [Senate Rule] classification is added.

## Implementation note
The paid leave contribution applies only to wages up to the pre-reform Social Security taxable maximum, indexed. It is counted toward the 60 percent ceiling but adds nothing at the top bracket, so it does not reduce the room reserved for the Universal Health Insurance contribution.

## Why
The platform's fiscal discipline makes a paid-for majority rule credible, and repeal symmetry protects enacted programs. Ranking only open-ended programs keeps the tier order focused on the costs that grow on their own.


**Note (decision 0033):** Tier 2 now lists universal preschool and the childcare cost caps as separate steps, with eldercare cost caps after them. Section 27.9 governs.
