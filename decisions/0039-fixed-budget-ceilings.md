## Question
Section 27.9 let the five fixed-budget programs expand "after the first tier is fully funded" but did not rank them against tiers 2 to 4, so they could compete with homelessness vouchers for the same revenue. None had a ceiling.

## Decision
- **F0, B.** Expansions above current-law levels begin only after tiers 1 and 2 are fully funded. They are paid from stable General Fund revenue ahead of tier 3, and never from the wealth-tax transfer.
- **Ceilings** (per year, indexed for inflation):
  - F1, research expansion: $8 billion (about $80 billion over ten years), closing the gap with CHIPS and Science Act research targets.
  - F2, national service: $1.25 billion (about $12.5 billion), roughly doubling AmeriCorps.
  - F3, National Farm Risk Protection Program enhancements: $3 billion (about $30 billion), designed to replace ad hoc disaster payments.
  - F4, public housing developer operations: $1 billion (about $10 billion); construction stays in the Capital Investment Account.
  - F5, state rent stabilization grants: $500 million (about $5 billion).

## Why
Placing expansions after tier 2 keeps the platform's order of priorities (income security, then housing and early childhood) unambiguous. The ceilings are large enough to matter and small enough, about $137 billion in all, to fit in the favorable case.

## Trade-off
In the conservative case the expansions wait, and research, national service, and the other programs stay at current-law levels. Critics will say a pro-growth platform has put research behind childcare.

Rule R2 in decision 0030 (expansion after tier 1).
