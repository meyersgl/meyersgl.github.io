## Question
How should the platform protect workers as automated decision systems and AI spread through the workplace, without slowing the technology itself?

## Decision
W1 to W11, all option A:
- **W1:** a federal statute on automated decisions at work, setting a minimum standard.
- **W2:** no firing, discipline, or deactivation based on an automated system alone.
- **W3:** notice, explanation, and data access.
- **W4:** vendor bias audits, with shared responsibility.
- **W5:** surveillance bans and quota disclosure. Refinement: an exception for voluntary assistive or accessibility technology requested by a worker with a disability, with strict limits on how that data is used and kept.
- **W6:** 90 days' notice of technological change, and mandatory bargaining over it.
- **W7:** Universal Adjustment Assistance, with wage insurance paid from unemployment trust funds.
- **W8:** BLS indicators of AI effects on jobs, with an apprenticeship trigger.
- **W9:** no tax on automation.
- **W10:** a federal minimum that does not preempt stronger state laws, with private and attorney-general enforcement.
- **W11:** training grants and triggered apprenticeships share one capped General Fund amount in tier 1, step 4 (now step 5 under decision 0032). A triggered expansion is paid within the cap and phases in if the cap is insufficient; raising the cap needs new scored revenue.

## Why
- The evidence shows harm concentrated on entry-level workers, not economy-wide displacement.
- Colorado's repeal showed that compliance-heavy regimes fail politically, so the design relies on rights workers can enforce themselves.
- A federal statute is the durable answer to Executive Order 14365.

## Consequences
Honest limits are in Section 3.6.12. The main ones:
- human review can become a rubber stamp;
- vendors will resist shared responsibility;
- the training budget is capped.
