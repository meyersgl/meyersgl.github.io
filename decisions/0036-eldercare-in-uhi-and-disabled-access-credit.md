## Question
Eldercare cost caps were listed as a General Fund program (tier 2, step 5), but Section 3.4 already sent long-term care through Universal Health Insurance, and Section 13.5.3 guarantees home- and community-based services within it. The cap could not be estimated apart from the UHI long-term care benefit, and as an unestimated step it blocked everything after it in the tier order. Separately, the Disabled Access Credit expansion had no parameters.

## Decision
- **E1, A. Eldercare is health care.** Long-term care for older adults is part of the UHI long-term care benefit (Section 5.1.1), with no copays or deductibles. Room and board in assisted living or nursing facilities is capped on a sliding scale. Family caregivers receive training, respite, and payment. All of it is paid by the UHI Trust Fund. Tier 2 no longer has an eldercare step.
- **E2, A.** The Disabled Access Credit covers digital accessibility, and its limit on eligible spending doubles from $10,250 to $20,500, indexed, for a credit of up to $10,000 a year (Section 13.5.1).

## Why
- One benefit, one payer: a separate General Fund cap would have duplicated the UHI benefit.
- Removing an unestimable step lets the affordability test run through every tier.
- The doubled limit matches the Disabled Access Credit Expansion Act (H.R. 5536, 115th Congress) and costs only a few billion dollars over ten years.

## Trade-offs
Eldercare now adds to the UHI Trust Fund, which has not been costed. Its cost will appear in the trust-fund tests (Appendix E.4), and a shortfall there slows UHI's phase-in rather than drawing on the General Fund (Section 2.8).

## Supersedes
The eldercare step in decisions 0030 and 0033.
