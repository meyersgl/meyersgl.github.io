## Question
Decision 0008 named the four federal tax fairness measures but set no rates. What should Chapter 2 propose, and how should the measures fit with the wealth tax, Social Security, and Universal Health Insurance?

## Options considered
Eight choices were decided together:
1. Top rate: restore 39.6% (chosen); add a surtax above $5M; or restore the whole pre-2018 schedule.
2. Capital gains threshold: ordinary rates above $1M of income (chosen); at the top-bracket threshold; or a 2-point increase only.
3. Gains at death: realization with a $5M per person exclusion and family-farm deferral (chosen); a $1M exclusion; or carryover basis.
4. Coordination rule so no gain is taxed twice across the wealth tax, gains at death, and the estate tax (adopted).
5. Corporate minimum tax: raise to 21% with research and production credits protected (chosen); also raise the regular rate to 25%; or tighten definitions only.
6. Estate tax: $5M indexed exemption, 40% rate, avoidance tools closed (chosen); $3.5M and 45%; or keep $15M and close loopholes only.
7. Publish proposed rates now, subject to scoring (chosen), or publish structure only.
8. Combined federal wage ceiling: 60% (chosen); disclosure only; or 55%.

## Decision
1A, 2A, 3A, 4, 5A, 6A, 7A, 8B, as written in Sections 2.6 to 2.12.

## Why
- A higher capital-gains rate raises revenue only if gains can no longer escape tax at death, so 2A and 3A go together.
- Eliminating the Social Security cap already raises the top rate on wages, so a further surtax (1B) would push combined rates too high.
- A ceiling fixed in law answers the "70 percent tax" attack with a number.
- Publishing rates is required for the combined-rate disclosure in Section 27.11.

## Consequences
- The Universal Health Insurance contribution on top wages is limited to the room under the ceiling (4.2 points at current rates). Any revenue the ceiling forgoes is made up inside the Trust Fund or by slower phase-in.
- Revenue is unscored. A shortfall slows program phase-in rather than raising rates.


**Note (decision 0034):** The wealth tax is now a tax on net worth, so item 4 no longer credits it against income tax. Section 2.6.1 phases out the pass-through deduction above the top bracket, and Section 2.7.5 adds a surtax on investment income; the wage ceiling is unchanged.
