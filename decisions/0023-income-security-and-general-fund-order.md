## Question
What should Chapter 4 contain, how should Medicaid fold into Universal Health Insurance, and where do these measures fall in the General Fund phase-in order?

## Options considered and decision
- **D1 Child Tax Credit:** C chosen. Full refundability of $2,200 plus a $1,000 supplement for children under 6, with a monthly payment option. Rejected: A, refundability only; B, the 2021 credit restored.
- **D2 Child Tax Credit eligibility:** C chosen. The child needs a Social Security number; the parent needs a Social Security number or lawful presence. Rejected: A, the pre-2025 rule; B, the 2025 rule.
- **D3 Earned Income Tax Credit:** A chosen. Expanded for workers without children on the 2021 model.
- **D4 Unemployment insurance:** A chosen. Federal minimum standards, a self-financing wage base, and automatic extended benefits paid from the Sovereign Wealth Reserve.
- **D5 SNAP:** B chosen, with additions. The work expectation applies to ages 18 to 54, with exemptions for people with disabilities (no federal disability determination required) and for caregivers (children under 6; sole caregivers of children under 14; caregivers of elderly or disabled relatives). Verification uses existing records first, with a 90-day self-attestation grace period. Error-rate penalties are replaced by performance bonuses.
- **D6 Medicaid:** A chosen. Work requirements repealed, plus a voluntary UHI Partnership Compact. Joining states pay 90 percent of baseline Medicaid spending and receive relief from long-term care costs and future cost growth. States that decline keep their existing funding. The compact is designed to avoid the coercion problem in *NFIB v. Sebelius*.
- **D7 SSI/SSDI:** A chosen. $10,000 and $20,000 indexed asset limits, updated income rules, and a $1-for-$2 earnings offset in Disability Insurance.
- **D8 Benefit cliffs:** A chosen. A 50 percent combined phase-out limit, one application, and an annual report.
- **D9 Phase-in order:** A chosen. Chapter 4 first (SSI and SNAP, then the EITC, then the Child Tax Credit), preschool and childcare caps second, tuition-free college third.

## Why
- These measures lift the most people out of poverty per dollar, and several are funded outside the General Fund.
- Putting them first answers the charge that the platform mainly helps the middle class.
- The voluntary compact keeps existing state funding safe, so it avoids the coercion problem.

## Consequences
- Tuition-free college starts later.
- Section 27.5 now lets Reserve draws pay for automatic extended unemployment benefits.
- The 50 percent limit and the compact's take-up are unscored risks, set out in Section 4.9.
