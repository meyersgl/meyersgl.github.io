## Question
How should the platform let people sue federal officers after *Egbert v. Boule*, and how should it help the local governments that carry liability costs once qualified immunity ends?

## Decision
- **C1: C.** One statutory cause of action for anyone acting under color of federal law, on the same terms as Section 1983 (no qualified immunity, capped personal share, government pays the rest). No categorical exception for immigration, border, or national security officers. Judgment Fund pays; the responsible agency repays it. Tort Claims Act repairs: unconstitutional conduct is never a discretionary function, the judgment bar is repealed, and Westfall Act exclusivity does not bar these claims.
- **C2: A.** Congress authorizes state converse-1983 remedies, limited to federal constitutional rights and no broader than the federal standard, with the same defenses and federal removal.
- **C3: B.** Civil Rights Risk Reduction Grants within the existing federal policing grant programs, with no new appropriation. Federal reinsurance rejected. Two-year transition before the new liability rules apply.
- **C3.5 (added after review):** mandatory public reporting of settlements, judgments, and fee awards to a Bureau of Justice Statistics database, with anonymous officer identifiers and no personal information.
- **C4: approved.** $25,000 cap indexed to inflation. No direct or indirect reimbursement by the employer (indemnification, pay adjustments, bonuses, or side agreements); personal insurance permitted. Bad faith (willful or malicious, found by a jury) removes the cap and allows punitive damages.
- **C5: approved.** Concurrent state attorney general authority under 34 U.S.C. 12601 and published reasons when the Justice Department abandons a case or ends a consent decree.

## Why
Ending qualified immunity only for state and local officers would leave federal officers more protected than anyone else. Limiting state remedies to the federal constitutional standard keeps them from being turned against lawful federal duties. Grants and public reporting work with insurance incentives rather than replacing them.
