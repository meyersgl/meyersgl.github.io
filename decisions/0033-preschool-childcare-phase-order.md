## Question
The tier 2 affordability test (Appendix E) showed that universal preschool and the childcare cost caps together cost about $560 to $870 billion over ten years. Under favorable assumptions only about $225 billion remains after tier 2, steps 1 and 2, less the training cap. Because a step takes effect only when fully funded, keeping both in one step would hold back preschool, the smaller and better-estimated program. Should the two be split?

## Decision
Option B. Tier 2 is now:
1. housing vouchers for people experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness (Section 8.3.3);
2. emergency rental assistance and the right to counsel (Section 8.3.6);
3. universal preschool for 4-year-olds (Section 7.1);
4. the childcare cost caps (Section 3.4);
5. the eldercare cost caps (Section 3.4).

The policies themselves are unchanged.

## Why
Preschool for 4-year-olds costs about $100 to $170 billion and fits within the favorable case if the training cap is no more than about $125 billion. Childcare caps cost about $460 to $700 billion and do not fit under current estimates in either case. The decision follows the same reasoning as decision 0032: the larger, less certain step should not hold back a smaller one that can take effect sooner.

## Options considered
- **A. Keep one step.** Kept the care agenda together, but neither program would take effect under current estimates.
- **C. Phase childcare caps by income.** Would reach the lowest-income families first, but requires redrafting Section 3.4 and is harder to cost. It can still be considered when the caps are designed.

## Trade-off
Critics may say the platform has delayed childcare. The answer is that under current estimates childcare caps wait either way; the split lets preschool begin first.

## Supersedes
The single "universal preschool and the childcare cost caps" step in decision 0030.
