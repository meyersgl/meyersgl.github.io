## Question
The costing in Appendix E showed known tier 1 to 3 costs of about $2.6 to $3.3 trillion over ten years against General Fund revenue of about $0.86 to $1.21 trillion. The platform chose to find revenue from the highest earners first and to narrow program scope only if needed (option D). Which measures, and how should wealth-tax revenue be used?

## Decision
- **R1, C. Wealth tax redesigned on the Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act:** 2 percent of net worth above $50 million, 3 percent above $1 billion, no large exemptions, a 40 percent exit tax, and a minimum audit rate. The gains-based design, the loan-as-realization rule, and the productive capital exemption are removed; Section 2.3 now governs valuation of illiquid assets.
- **Revenue use: smoothed transfer.** Revenue still fills the Sovereign Wealth Reserve first. Once the Reserve is at its target, the General Fund receives each year one-half of the lowest annual receipts in the five preceding years, usable only for tiers 3 and 4. Unneeded amounts stay in the Reserve. The rest goes to the Capital Investment Account.
- **R2, B.** The pass-through deduction (Section 199A) phases out above the top-bracket threshold (Section 2.6.1).
- **R3.** The enforcement division in Section 2.4 receives $80 billion of mandatory funding over ten years; Appendix E counts its net revenue at CBO's estimated return.
- **R4, B.** A 10 percent surtax on net investment income above $1 million ($2 million joint), excluding wages, so the 60 percent wage ceiling is unchanged (Section 2.7.5).

## Why
- A tax on net worth raises several times more than a tax on gains alone, and the highest fortunes have roughly doubled since 2019.
- Wealth-tax revenue still swings with markets. Filling the Reserve first, smoothing the transfer on the lowest recent year, and limiting it to tiers 3 and 4 keep that volatility, and the risk of a court ruling against the tax, away from tiers 1 and 2.
- The pass-through phase-out and the surtax reach only top-bracket and millionaire households. Excluding wages from the surtax keeps the 60 percent ceiling in decision 0022.

## Options considered
- R1: keep the gains-based tax (A or B), or switch to a net-worth tax (C, chosen). Uses: keep decision 0004, smoothed transfer (chosen), or a fixed share to the General Fund for any tier.
- R2: no change, phase-out above the top bracket (chosen), or full repeal.
- R4: no surtax, surtax on investment income only (chosen), or surtax on all income with a 65 percent wage ceiling.

## Trade-offs
- An annual net-worth tax carries the highest constitutional risk in the platform (Direct Tax Clause). The severability rule limits the damage to tiers 3 and 4.
- With the surtax, the top rate on gains is 53.4 percent, above the revenue-maximizing estimate of about 42 percent; the surtax may raise little from gains.
- In the conservative case tier 2 still does not finish, so the wealth-tax transfer cannot yet be used.

## Supersedes
Decision 0004 in part: wealth-tax revenue may now reach the General Fund through the smoothed transfer, for tiers 3 and 4 only. Decision 0022, item 4: the coordination rule no longer credits wealth tax against income tax, because the wealth tax is no longer a tax on gains.
