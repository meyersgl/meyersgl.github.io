## Question
How should Chapter 13 protect voting rights, equal treatment, disability rights, and equal pay so that they survive hostile court rulings, *Trump v. Slaughter*, and executive rollback?

## Decision
V1 A, V2 B (with C listed in Appendix A), V3 A, V4 A, D1 A as refined, D2 A, D3 A as refined, D4 A, DR1 A, DR2 A, DR3 B, P1 A, P2 A, P3 A, E1 A, E2 A, G1 A as refined.

Refinements:
- **D1:** sex, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, pregnancy, and sex characteristics are separate protected traits with separate definitions. They are not folded into "sex."
- **D3:**
  - For-profit corporations do not exercise religion under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act or the civil rights statutes, reversing *Hobby Lobby*.
  - Exemptions are limited to houses of worship, bona fide religious nonprofits, and the ministerial exception.
  - The *303 Creative* protection for compelled expressive work is respected.
- **G1:** the expanded Disabled Access Credit goes in tier 1, alongside Chapter 4.
- **DR2:** home- and community-based services are tied to the Universal Health Insurance long-term care benefit in Section 5.1.7.

## Why
- *Callais* rests on the Constitution, so durability comes from race-neutral remedies and evidentiary rules, not from restoring the old test.
- Rights written into statute and enforceable by the people protected survive both executive rollback and the limits *Slaughter* places on agency independence.

## Consequences
- Honest limits are recorded in Section 13.9. The main ones are:
  - *303 Creative* applies to businesses as well as individuals;
  - the free-exercise "general applicability" argument;
  - constitutional pressure on disparate impact.
